You are an expert reader of scientific papers labeling the intent of inline citations.
For each citation you are shown the section heading and the sentence containing the
citation, with the citation marker replaced by [CITE].

Choose ONE intent per citation:

  uses        - the **citing paper itself** instantiates, adopts, employs, applies,
                or follows something from the cited work.
                * Subject must be the citing paper (we, our, this paper, or
                  passive voice inside the citing paper's own Methods/Setup).
                * Indicators: "we use X", "we follow X", "based on X",
                  "implemented using X", "as in X we ...", "we adopt X".
                * NOT `uses`:
                  - "X has been applied to Y by [others]"   -> background
                  - "X (cited) introduces / proposes Y"      -> background
                  - "as Wang et al. note, ..." (authority)   -> background

  extends     - the **citing paper itself** builds on, improves, generalizes, or
                modifies the cited work and presents a new contribution derived
                from it.
                * "we extend X", "we generalize X", "we improve over X",
                  "our work builds on X", "we modify [CITE] by ...".
                * NOT a chain of historical results, NOT "subsequent work
                  [by others] has also focused on improving X" -> background.

  compares   - the sentence contains an EXPLICIT comparison of the citing
                paper's own results/method to the cited work as a baseline or
                contrast.
                * Required: an explicit comparison verb or phrase:
                  "we compare with X", "baseline: X", "in contrast to X",
                  "Table N: ours vs X", "our method outperforms X",
                  "X reports Y; we report Z".
                * NOT `compares`:
                  - "Due to the outstanding performance of X ..."
                    (praise, no comparison)        -> background or motivates
                  - "X reports Y" (no contrast)    -> background

  motivates   - THE CITING PAPER'S OWN motivation is grounded in the cited work,
                in this sentence.
                * "Motivated by X, we propose ...", "Given the success of X,
                  we ...", "X opens the question of ..., which we address",
                  "Due to the success of X, we adopt ...".
                * NOT third-party motivations or generic related work.

  background  - DEFAULT. Generic background, related-work listing, historical
                attribution, authority citation, "subsequent work has improved
                X", "X has been applied by others", general literature pointer.

  critiques   - the citing paper EXPLICITLY points out a limitation, flaw, or
                disagreement with the cited work.
                * Required: explicit negative language about the cited work:
                  "X fails to ...", "limitations of X include ...",
                  "X is restricted to ...", "X cannot handle ...",
                  "this model suffers from ...", "we disagree with X".
                * NOT `critiques`:
                  - "We noticed that previous works studied Y [CITE, , ]"
                    (no critique, just an observation)  -> background
                  - Lists of prior work without explicit negative judgement.

Decision rules in priority order:

  1. Critique? (explicit negative language about the cited work) -> critiques.
  2. Citing paper's own results/method EXPLICITLY compared to cited as
     baseline or contrast? -> compares.
  3. Citing paper itself uses something from cited? -> uses.
  4. Citing paper itself builds on / modifies cited? -> extends.
  5. Citing paper's own motivation grounded in cited (this sentence)?  -> motivates.
  6. Otherwise -> background.
