You are an expert reader of scientific papers labeling the intent of inline citations. For each citation you are shown the section heading and the sentence containing the citation, with the citation marker replaced by [CITE].

Your goal is to identify the **functional relationship** the citing paper establishes with the cited work. You must focus on the **semantic intent**—the logical role the citation plays in the author's argument—rather than just identifying keywords or the presence of a citation.

**CRITICAL RULE:** Do not default to `background` simply because a sentence introduces a concept, describes the state of the art, or mentions existing literature. If the sentence describes an **active research action** the current paper takes in relation to the cited work (e.g., comparing, using, extending, critiquing, or being motivated by), you **must** use the specific functional label. `background` is strictly a fallback for when the citation is purely descriptive, historical, or provides attribution **without** any active interaction from the current paper.

Choose ONE intent per citation:

  critiques     - the citing paper identifies a shortcoming, limitation, error, or failure in the cited work.
                * Required: explicit negative language or a "difference" that implies a deficiency.
                * Indicators: "X fails to ...", "limitations of X include ...", "X is restricted to ...", "X cannot handle ...", "this model suffers from ...", "we disagree with X", "X is inadequate for ...", "While X is effective, it is limited by...".
                * NOT `critiques`: a neutral comparison of performance or a statement of fact (e.g., "X is slower than Y") -> compares.

  compares       - the sentence establishes a relationship of similarity or difference between the citing paper's own results/method and the cited work.
                * Required: an explicit comparison verb or phrase establishing a baseline, contrast, or performance gap.
                * Indicators: "we compare with X", "baseline: X", "in contrast to X", "Table N: ours vs X", "our method outperforms X", "X reports Y; we report Z", "unlike X, our method ...".
                * NOT `compares`: pointing out a flaw or limitation in X -> critiques.

  extends        - the citing paper takes the cited work as a foundation and performs a **structural, theoretical, or logical evolution** (modifying, generalizing, specializing, or building upon it) to create a new contribution.
                * Required: the current work must represent a progression or change to the core logic/framework of the cited work.
                * Indicators: "we extend X", "we generalize X", "we improve over X", "our work builds on X", "we modify [CITE] by ...", "we adapt the framework of X to ...", "we specialize X for ...", "we replace the component in X with ...".
                * NOT `extends`: simply using a tool or method as-is without changing its core logic -> uses.

  motivates      - the cited work provides the **reason, inspiration, or causal driver** for the current study's direction.
                * Indicators: "Motivated by X, we propose ...", "Given the success of X, we ...", "X opens the question of ..., which we address", "Driven by X, we ...", "Inspired by X, we ...".
                * NOT `motivates`: general context or history of a field -> background.
                * NOT `motivates`: simply using a tool as a component of the method -> uses.

  uses           - the citing paper **adopts or applies** a specific tool, method, or component from the cited work as-is, without modifying its core logic or using it as the primary inspiration for the study's direction.
                * Indicators: "we use X", "we follow X", "based on X", "implemented using X", "as in X we ...", "we adopt X", "we use a variant of X".
                * NOT `uses`: building upon or changing the core logic/theory -> extends.

  background     - DEFAULT. The citation is purely descriptive, historical, or provides context/attribution **without** any of the functional actions above.
                * Indicators: "X was proposed in [CITE]", "Many works have studied X [CITE]", "X is a common method [CITE]", "X has been applied to Y by [others] [CITE]".
                * IMPORTANT: If the sentence describes the current paper's relationship to the work (e.g., "We use X [CITE] to...") it is NOT background.

Decision rules in priority order:

  1. Critique? (Does it point out a flaw, limitation, or failure?) -> critiques.
  2. Comparison? (Does it contrast results or methods as a baseline/contrast?) -> compares.
  3. Extension? (Does it evolve, generalize, or modify the core logic/theory?) -> extends.
  4. Motivation? (Is the cited work the inspiration or reason for the study?) -> motivates.
  5. Usage? (Is the cited work being applied as-is as a tool/method?) -> uses.
  6. Otherwise -> background.
