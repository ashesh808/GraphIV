\section*{What we did}
Four query-embedding conditions, full pipeline each (graph drill $+$ PPR $+$ compose), judged
end-to-end over the 3 gold topics: \texttt{sym}/\texttt{asym} (symmetric vs nomic asymmetric
\texttt{search\_query}/\texttt{search\_document}) $\times$ \texttt{hyde}/\texttt{raw} (a HyDE
hypothetical-document expansion vs the raw question). The committed choice is \texttt{asym\_raw}.

\section*{Conclusion}
\textbf{The committed \texttt{asym\_raw} ties for best} --- landmark recall \asymRecall{}, spine
recall \asymSpine{}, top judge --- at \emph{no} extra generation. \textbf{\texttt{sym\_raw} is the
worst} (recall \symrawRecall{}): a symmetric embedding on a bare question under-retrieves the
document space. \textbf{HyDE rescues the symmetric space} (\texttt{sym\_hyde} recall \symhydeRecall{},
matching asym) but \textbf{adds nothing to the asymmetric space} (\texttt{asym\_hyde} $\leq$
\texttt{asym\_raw}) while costing a hypothetical-document generation per query. So the asymmetric
query/document encoding already does what HyDE does --- aligns the query into document space --- making
HyDE redundant. \textbf{Ship \texttt{asym\_raw}}: best recall, no HyDE latency. \textbf{Caveat:} n$=3$
gold; the judge axes are compressed, but recall/spine (objective) agree with the judge here.
