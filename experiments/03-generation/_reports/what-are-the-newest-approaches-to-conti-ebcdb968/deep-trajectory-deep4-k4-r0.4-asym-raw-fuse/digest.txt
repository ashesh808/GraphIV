DEEP CRITIQUES (full-text peer review of key papers):
arXiv:1703.04200:
NOVELTY: Introduction of "intelligent synapses" that utilize an online, path-integral-based importance measure ($\omega_k^\mu$) to consolidate parameters, rather than relying on post-hoc Fisher information estimates.

STRENGTHS: Demonstrates significant resistance to forgetting on Permuted MNIST (Fig. 4) and CIFAR-10/100 (Fig. 6), maintaining accuracy comparable to training on all data simultaneously.

WEAKNESSES: The method relies on a hyperparameter $c$ (consolidation strength) that requires empirical tuning (e.g., via grid search in Section 5.2) and introduces a damping parameter $\xi$ to prevent division by zero.

RELEVANCE: Provides a structural regularization approach to continual learning that moves beyond static weight freezing by treating synapses as dynamic entities that track their own contribution to loss reduction.
arXiv:2601.05623:
NOVELTY: Proposes ETCL, a non-expansion method that explicitly optimizes for both forgetting-free learning (via task-specific binary masks and sparse sub-networks) and positive knowledge transfer (FKT/BKT) through initial gradient alignment and bi-objective classification layer updates.

STRENGTHS: Provides a theoretical framework for KT bounds (Theorem 1) and introduces an online task similarity detector (SDM) that uses Wasserstein distance between representation bases, avoiding the need for old task data.

WEAKNESSES: The claim of "forgetting-free" learning is potentially undermined by the BKT mechanism, which updates weights of previous tasks; while the paper uses orthogonal gradient projection (GPM) to mitigate this, the risk of interference in the classification layer remains a critical point of failure.

RELEVANCE: Directly addresses the research gap where most continual learning methods focus solely on mitigating catastrophic forgetting while ignoring the potential for positive forward and backward knowledge transfer.
arXiv:2602.05454:
NOVELTY: Identifies "attention drift" in Vision Transformers as a primary driver of forgetting and proposes a data-free, regularization-based method (ARCL-ViT) that uses layer-wise rollout and adaptive thresholding to generate gradient masks.

STRENGTHS: Achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks, notably outperforming VPT-CPG by 3.3% accuracy in 50-split ImageNet-R (Table 5) and reducing attention drift to 6.0% (Figure 1b).

WEAKNESSES: The method relies on an "instance-adaptive" thresholding strategy (Eq. 10) that requires careful implementation of second-order derivatives, and the theoretical justification for why gradient masking approximates the attention-retention objective is relegated to an appendix.

RELEVANCE: Provides a specific, architecture-aware approach for Vision Transformers that moves beyond general parameter regularization by targeting the internal attention mechanisms used in modern CL models.
