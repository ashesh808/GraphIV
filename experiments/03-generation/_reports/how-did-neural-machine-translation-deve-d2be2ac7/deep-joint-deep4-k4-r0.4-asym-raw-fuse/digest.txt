DEEP CRITIQUES (full-text peer review of key papers):
arXiv:1409.0473:
NOVELTY: Replaces the fixed-length vector bottleneck in encoder-decoder architectures with a "soft-alignment" mechanism that adaptively selects relevant source annotations for each target word.
STRENGTHS: Demonstrates significant robustness to sentence length compared to basic RNN encoder-decoders (Fig 2) and achieves BLEU scores comparable to phrase-based systems (Table 1).
WEAKNESSES: The computational requirement of scoring every source word against every target word may limit scalability, and the model still struggles with unknown/rare words.
RELEVANCE: Marks the critical transition from compressing entire sentences into single vectors to using attention-based retrieval, providing the conceptual foundation for Transformer-based systems.
arXiv:1508.07909:
NOVELTY: Replaces fixed-vocabulary NMT constraints and back-off dictionaries with an open-vocabulary approach using Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) for subword segmentation.
STRENGTHS: Demonstrates empirical improvements in translation quality, specifically increasing BLEU by up to 1.3 on English $\rightarrow$ Russian tasks.
WEAKNESSES: The methodology is tied to RNN-based architectures (Bahdanau et al., 2015) and does not resolve the observed performance variability in NMT training.
RELEVANCE: Provides a foundational solution to the "open-vocabulary problem," enabling the transition from restrictive word-level NMT to the flexible subword-centric modeling used in modern systems.
