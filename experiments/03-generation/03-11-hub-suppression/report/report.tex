\section*{The problem}
Reading the real deep-research outputs, the flagship's dominant rough edge is \textbf{generic-hub
dominance}: ``Attention Is All You Need'' is cited in \textbf{27 of 27} deep reports, BERT in 17, GPT-3
in 16, the Adam optimizer in 11 --- regardless of topic. A theorem-proving review devotes a whole
``Rise of LLMs and Transformers'' section to Attention$\rightarrow$BERT$\rightarrow$GPT-3 and cites
\emph{Adam as foundational to theorem proving}. Root cause: these hubs accrue high \emph{in-topic}
in-degree (nearly every modern paper cites them), so PPR/in-degree floats them to top landmarks. This
is a \textbf{specificity} problem, not a similarity one --- and it must be fixed without a blocklist.

\section*{Data-derived signal sweep (no blocklist)}
We swept every plausible \emph{data-derived} demotion signal --- global IDF, topic-lift
(P(cited$\mid$topic)$/$P(cited)), citation-intent background-ratio, a
personalized-PR$/$global-popularity penalty, and \texttt{pop\_bg} (demote only papers that are BOTH
globally popular AND background-cited \emph{here}, topic-conditioned, strength $s$). Two metrics over
27 topics: \textbf{gold\_recall} (canonical retention --- the tripwire) and an \textbf{independent
LLM oracle} that labels each paper specific/generic \emph{for that topic} (it never sees our signals).

\section*{Finding: a real frontier, and no structural silver bullet}
There is a genuine recall$\leftrightarrow$specificity \textbf{frontier}. The topic-conditioned
\texttt{pop\_bg} \textbf{holds gold recall perfectly} ($\noneRecall\rightarrow\popRecall$ at $s\le0.7$)
because it never penalizes a load-bearing paper by fame alone --- but it is \emph{gentle} (oracle
generic-frac only $\noneGeneric\rightarrow\popGeneric$). Topic-blind penalties (\texttt{pr\_ratio})
move the oracle most ($\noneGeneric\rightarrow\prGeneric$) but cost recall
($\noneRecall\rightarrow\prRecall$, concentrated on \texttt{transformer}, where the hubs genuinely
\emph{are} canonical). \textbf{Crucially, no purely-structural signal removes a hub that is genuinely
\emph{used} in the niche} --- Attention stays a landmark in theorem-proving at every strength, because
ATP papers really do use transformers (low background-ratio there). So ``generic for this topic'' is
partly a \textbf{narrative} judgment, not a structural one.

\section*{The fix: a compose-side guard (shipped, A/B-validated)}
Since retrieval cannot cleanly remove a legitimately-present-but-generic paper, the fix is a
one-line compose guard (\texttt{compose\_report.\_SYS} + \texttt{long\_compose.\_OUTLINE\_SYS})
forbidding a generic-foundations section unless directly load-bearing. \textbf{Deep A/B on
``RL for automated theorem proving'':}

\noindent Re-validated on three further niche topics (firm-network causal propagation; two
document-layout-parsing variants): \textbf{zero} generic-hub mentions, all sections topic-specific,
coverage intact --- not over-corrected. \textbf{The recall-safe \texttt{pop\_bg} retrieval lever
remains available} (behind a knob) for the milder generic-\emph{method} residue in ultra-niche topics.

\section*{Caveat}
The oracle is an LLM instrument (validated style of \texttt{03-01}); gold recall is n$=3$.
\texttt{pop\_bg}'s structural metrics move modestly because it is deliberately gentle --- its value is
zero-recall-cost cleanup of truly-background hubs, complementary to the compose guard.
