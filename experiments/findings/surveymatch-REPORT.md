# surveymatch

Four benchmarks for the retrieval and reranking step, the ablation that says what each part of the
score contributes, and the profile that says whether a miss matters.

`REPORT_BENCH.md` holds every table here at full width and is regenerated by `report.py` from
`results/bench/`, never edited by hand. `REPORT_clique.md` is the earlier leaf-clique case
selection, which now feeds the ConsensusMatch triples.

## Configuration

| item | value |
| --- | --- |
| corpus | 3,164,437 papers, 3,128,573 placed in the topic tree |
| citation graph | 73,335,201 resolvable occurrences, 38,159,685 distinct edges |
| vector | `papers.embed_full`, halfvec(768), unit normalized |
| query embedding | nomic-embed-text-v2-moe, `search_query: ` prefix, no fine tune |
| seed | cosine top 5,000 under the case's date cap, `hnsw.iterative_scan = relaxed_order` |
| expansion | one citation hop each way off the top 200 of the seed, 20,000 per direction |
| score | `cos^C * rec^C * prod(1 + lg(channel))^C / (1 + lg(cited))^C_HUB`, every term on `[1, 2^C]` |
| channels | ancestors `A@w`, frontier `A.T@w`, co-citation `A@A.T@w`, coupling `A.T@A@w` |
| citations | counted as of the cutoff, with the case's own surveys excluded |
| blocklist | the case's own surveys, out of the pool and out of every citation count |
| llm | none, anywhere in retrieval or reranking |

## Case construction

| metric | case | query | cutoff | answer key |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SurveyMatch | one survey | its title | its own date | its resolved references dated before it |
| FrontierMatch | two surveys in one leaf, gap <= 190 days, cosine >= 0.80 | S1's title | S2's date | S2's references absent from S1, dated in `[S1 - 2 years, S2]` |
| ConsensusMatch | three surveys in one leaf, span <= 190 days, pairwise cosine >= 0.75 | all three titles | earliest of the three | what all three cite, dated at or before the cutoff |
| PrecisionMatch | the SurveyMatch cases | same | same | none, the top 1,000 is classified instead |

Every survey in a case needs 25 or more resolved in-corpus references, and no survey appears in two
cases of the same metric.

### The strongest subset against the shipped one

| metric | best subset | its recall@1000 | full model |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| survey | cos+anc+fro+cocite+hub | 0.606 | 0.578 |
| frontier | cos+rec+anc+fro+cocite+hub | 0.576 | 0.572 |
| consensus | cos+rec+anc+fro+cocite | 0.962 | 0.941 |

4) Two channels carry every result, and which of them leads depends on the question: ancestors when
   the key is the field's own history, co-citation when the key is what appeared next.
5) Cosine's marginal is third on SurveyMatch and ConsensusMatch and fifth on FrontierMatch, never
   first or second, because the pool is already a cosine seed and cosine inside the score only
   reorders what cosine selected.
6) Coupling, the frontier channel, recency and the hub denominator all carry a positive mean
   marginal and all lose the sign test on SurveyMatch, which is the signature of a term that helps
   when it is the only graph signal present and hurts once ancestors and co-citation are there.
7) Those same four terms are statistically neutral on FrontierMatch, every p above 0.07, so they are
   not universally harmful, they are mode dependent.
8) Dropping coupling takes SurveyMatch from 0.578 to 0.606, and the shipped constants are not the
   best point in the lattice on any metric, which follows directly from their never having been
   fitted to one.

## The exponents, tuned

The lattice switches terms on and off at their shipped values and never varies those values.
`tune.py` sweeps the values themselves: coordinate descent over a five point grid per term, three
rounds, then 600 random joint samples, all on half the cases, reported on the other half.

| metric | budget | arm | `cos`/`rec`/`anc`/`fro`/`cocite`/`couple`/`hub` | tuning | held out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| survey | 1000 | shipped | 0.5/1.0/2.0/1.0/1.0/1.0/0.3 | 0.5589 | 0.5974 |
| survey | 1000 | tuned | 1.0/0.25/2.0/0.5/4.0/1.0/0.0 | 0.5983 | **0.6364** |
| frontier | 1000 | shipped | 0.5/1.0/2.0/1.0/1.0/1.0/0.3 | 0.5580 | 0.5857 |
| frontier | 1000 | tuned, coordinate | 1.0/1.0/1.0/0.25/2.0/1.0/1.5 | 0.5876 | 0.6043 |
| frontier | 1000 | tuned, random | 2.0/2.0/2.0/1.0/4.0/1.0/0.15 | 0.5877 | **0.6127** |
| consensus | 100 | shipped | 0.5/1.0/2.0/1.0/1.0/1.0/0.3 | 0.6200 | 0.6697 |
| consensus | 100 | tuned | 1.0/2.0/4.0/0.5/1.0/0.0/0.15 | 0.7466 | **0.7900** |

| term | survey wants | frontier wants | consensus wants |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cosine | 1.0 | 1.0 to 2.0 | 0.5 to 1.0 |
| recency | 0.25 | 1.0 to 2.0 | 2.0 |
| ancestors | 2.0 | 1.0 to 2.0 | 4.0 |
| frontier | 0.5 | 0.25 to 1.0 | 0.5 |
| co-citation | 4.0 | 2.0 to 4.0 | 0.5 to 1.0 |
| coupling | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0.0 |
| hub | 0.0 | 0.15 to 1.5 | 0.15 to 0.7 |

21) Tuning the values is worth +0.039 on SurveyMatch, +0.027 on FrontierMatch and +0.120 on
    ConsensusMatch over the shipped point, all on cases the search never saw.
22) The hub exponent is the term that splits hardest by mode, 0.0 when the key is the field's own
    history and up to 1.5 when it is what appeared next, so it is a mode switch rather than a
    constant, which is what `docs/decisions.md` already says about lineage and frontier.
23) Recency splits the same way and in the same direction, 0.25 against 1.0 to 2.0.
24) Co-citation wants the top of its grid on two of three metrics, so 4.0 is a floor on its true
    value and not an optimum.
25) Coordinate descent converged in one round on every metric and 600 random joint samples beat it
    only on FrontierMatch and only by 0.0001 on the tuning half, so the objective is smooth and one
    pass finds it.

## Whether a miss matters

A reference other reviewers on the same leaf also cite is one the field agrees on. The case's own
surveys are excluded from that count.

| subset of the answer key | SurveyMatch papers | recall | FrontierMatch papers | recall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| every reference | 25,648 | 0.537 | 10,839 | 0.501 |
| two or more other reviewers cite it | 5,051 | 0.816 | 2,528 | 0.783 |
| the same, inside the pool | 4,778 | 0.862 | 2,383 | 0.831 |
| no other reviewer cites it | 16,737 | 0.427 | 6,347 | 0.363 |

9) 65% of a bibliography is cited by no other reviewer on the same topic, so most of what a
   recall-against-one-survey metric asks for is that author's own selection rather than the field's.
10) The headline is the product of two limits that are not the ranker: 0.760 of the key is reachable
    in one citation hop, and the ranker returns 0.707 of what it can reach.
11) On the papers the field agrees on, which is what a review has to find, the number is 0.816 and
    0.862 once candidate generation is held fixed.

    unrelated verdict and the union only rescues papers the tree placed elsewhere.

    measures what reviewers of that topic reach for, and the two disagree.
14) A random thousand papers dated before the cutoff is 91.7% strictly unrelated against the full
    model's 18.8% in the top hundred.

## Whether the three modes are three answers

The exponents were tuned on three different answer keys. The question is whether that produces
three retrievals or one retrieval read three ways. Five topics, top 20 each.

| topic | how we got here | what is agreed | what is new | jaccard of the three |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| retrieval augmented generation | 2023 / 879 | 2023 / 793 | 2023 / 732 | 0.640 |
| protein structure prediction | 2022 / 80 | 2022 / 80 | 2022 / 61 | 0.577 |
| gravitational wave detection | 2019 / 916 | 2018 / 1023 | 2020 / 628 | 0.367 |
| graph neural networks for molecules | 2019 / 899 | 2019 / 488 | 2019 / 899 | 0.538 |
| differentially private training | 2020 / 346 | 2019 / 346 | 2021 / 262 | 0.600 |

Cells are the median year and median citation count of the top 20.

| pair | mean overlap of the top 20 |
| --- | --- |

5) The modes move the median year by at most two years and the median citation count by about a
   quarter, and they share 72% to 84% of their top twenty, so they reorder one neighborhood rather
   than retrieving three.
6) On retrieval-augmented generation the frontier mode returns a 2023 paper with 760 citations at
   rank one, which is the canon and not the frontier.
7) The cause is structural: every graph channel is a weighted walk from a jury of the top 200 by
   cosine, so a paper nothing has cited yet scores zero on all four of them and no exponent can
   lift it. FrontierMatch did not catch this, because its key is what a later survey cited, and by
   then those papers had citations.

## The second citation hop, and what the grandparent count is actually for

The 0.240 of every answer key beyond one hop was the largest single limit in this report. The rule
tested is: admit a distance-two paper when at least N distinct one-hop papers reach it, which is
its grandparent count inside the pool.

Nine admission rules, 25 cases, ranked by key papers recovered per thousand candidates admitted.

| rule | candidates p50 | p90 | key recovered | share of the key | per 1,000 candidates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| grandparents >= 5 | 10,184 | 56,221 | 239 | 0.119 | 0.94 |
| grandparents >= 3 and cited 5 or more | 22,939 | 72,200 | 272 | 0.135 | 0.47 |
| grandparents >= 2 and cited 20 or more | 22,773 | 49,953 | 248 | 0.123 | 0.44 |
| in-degree 50 or more | 19,243 | 33,272 | 181 | 0.090 | 0.38 |
| grandparents >= 3 | 32,244 | 107,423 | 293 | 0.145 | 0.36 |
| in-degree 20 or more | 41,842 | 71,470 | 273 | 0.135 | 0.26 |
| grandparents >= 2 | 52,723 | 156,251 | 330 | 0.164 | 0.25 |
| everything at distance two | 104,405 | 262,526 | 415 | 0.206 | 0.16 |
| in-degree under 2 | 14,904 | 58,259 | 12 | 0.006 | 0.03 |

The key papers at distance two are not obscure, which is why in-degree is the wrong axis: 0.436 of
them carry 20 or more citations at the cutoff and only 0.157 carry fewer than two.

26) Admitting the candidates lifts the ceiling by 0.089 and recall by 0.0005, so candidate
    generation was not the binding constraint at a 1,000 slot budget, ranking was.
27) `ANC` and `FRO` are one-step walks from a 200 paper jury, so a paper two hops out scores

28) The grandparent count was being spent as an admission filter and thrown away. Spent instead as
    a channel, `ANC2 = A @ A @ w`, it is worth +0.017 at 1,000 slots and +0.025 at 500, for two
    extra sparse matvecs and no change to the pool or the query time.

## Distance to the jury, instead of a one-step walk

`ANC` and `FRO` are `A @ w` and `A.T @ w`, so they are exactly zero for any paper the jury does not
touch directly, and `1 + lg(0)` is 1 whatever the paper is. `ANCD` replaces the cliff with a decay:
a multi-source breadth-first walk from the same 200 paper jury, giving `DECAY ** distance` out to
four steps and zero beyond. The term enters the score raw rather than through `lg`, since a decay
curve is already on [0, 1] and `lg` would rescale it onto its own maximum.

| full plus `ANCD` 2.0 | 0.239 | 0.494 | 0.602 | +0.0236 | 174 | 81 | 0.0000 |

31) `ANCD` gains significantly on one metric and loses significantly on none, which no other change
    in this report does, so it is the one point that clears the bar to become the default.
32) Replacing the one-step channels with distance loses 0.023, so the two carry different things:
    the walk weights by jury cosine and by how many jury papers arrive, the distance does not
    weight at all and only says how far away the paper is.
33) The forward distance loses 0.024 on the same cases, so the asymmetry between `ANC` and `FRO`
    survives the change of transform and is a property of the direction rather than of the form.
34) The result is flat in `DECAY` across 0.3 to 0.7, so what the channel contributes is reachability
    within four steps and not the shape of the discount.
35) `ANC2` is the distance-two term taken alone and weighted, and pairing it with `ANCD` is the best
    arm on SurveyMatch at 0.613, but `ANC2` costs FrontierMatch 0.0096, so that pairing is a lineage
    mode setting where `ANCD` alone is not.

## Genuinely open, with the measurement that says so

| open problem | the number behind it | ours or the field's |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a retrieval signal for a paper nothing has cited yet | the three modes share 0.72 to 0.84 of their top twenty and the frontier mode returns a 760-citation 2023 paper at rank one | ours, and unsolved here |
| reaching hop two usefully | admitting the candidates buys 0.0005; scoring by distance to the jury instead buys 0.0265 without admitting anything, so the remaining question is what else the pool already holds and cannot rank | ours, narrowed twice by this run |
| a query that can express a constraint | "instead of a language model" retrieves language-model rerankers, because a dense score has no negation | ours, and general to dense retrieval |
| what recall should be scored against | 65% of a bibliography is cited by no other reviewer on the topic, and two human experts agree at 0.265 | ours, and `2002.06961` names evaluation as the open problem in citation recommendation |
| choosing the mode | two exponents switch lineage against frontier and nothing picks them from the query | ours |
| whether the topic tree should come from the citations | the reranker sits below cosine on every tree test and 2.3x above it on the leaf citation union, and `2605.07158` puts embeddings at 15% to 21% same-agenda | both |
| benchmarks that do not go stale | `2508.20033` names staleness and contamination, `2508.15658` and `2505.21815` each define their own evaluation, and 105 papers in the earlier read named 105 distinct evaluations used by none twice | the field's |
| citation quality in a generated survey | `2508.17647` names low citation quality and limited critical analysis as what fully automatic generation suffers from | the field's |
| cold start, sparsity and serendipity in paper recommendation | `2008.13538` names all three, and they are the same failure as the uncited-paper problem above | both |
