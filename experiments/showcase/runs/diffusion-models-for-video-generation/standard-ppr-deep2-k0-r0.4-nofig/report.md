# From Temporal Sequences to Generative Diffusion: A Survey of Video Synthesis Evolution

The ability to synthesize realistic, high-fidelity video remains one of the most significant challenges in computer vision and generative artificial intelligence. Unlike static image synthesis, video generation requires the simultaneous modeling of complex spatial structures and intricate temporal dynamics, necessitating a robust understanding of the foundational principles within the Foundations of Generative Modeling and Video Representation. As the demand for automated content creation grows across digital media, robotics, and virtual simulations, the ability to produce motion that is both visually convincing and semantically coherent has become a critical driver of technological innovation.

This review traces the technological trajectory of temporal synthesis, beginning with the historical limitations of Early Video Generation: GANs and Sequence Models, which often struggled with temporal instability and limited diversity. We then examine the transformative paradigm shift brought about by The Rise of Video Diffusion Models, which leveraged iterative denoising processes to achieve unprecedented levels of detail. The discussion evolves to address the essential requirements for Controllability and Motion-Aware Synthesis, ensuring that generated movements align with specific user intents. Furthermore, we explore the ongoing efforts regarding Scaling, Efficiency, and Long-Term Consistency to overcome the computational bottlenecks inherent in high-resolution temporal modeling. Finally, the review concludes by examining The Current Frontier: Transformers and Advanced Video Diffusion, where the integration of large-scale architectural innovations is redefining the boundaries of generative video synthesis.

## Foundations of Generative Modeling and Video Representation

The evolution of generative modeling has transitioned from early variational frameworks to sophisticated diffusion-based architectures. Initial progress was driven by the need to handle intractable posterior distributions in directed probabilistic models with continuous latent variables, a challenge addressed by the introduction of stochastic variational inference and the reparameterization of the variational lower bound in arXiv:1312.6114. This foundation enabled the optimization of complex models via standard stochastic gradient methods.

Parallel to variational approaches, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) introduced a minimax two-player game between a generator and a discriminator (arXiv:1406.2661). While GANs offered a different paradigm, they faced significant stability issues, specifically a lack of proven convergence during training. This was partially mitigated by the Two Time-Scale Update Rule (TTUR), which employs individual learning rates for the generator and discriminator to facilitate convergence to a local Nash equilibrium (arXiv:1706.08500). Further refinements in GAN architectures, such as the style-based generator (arXiv:1812.04948), improved image synthesis by enabling the unsupervised separation of high-level attributes and stochastic variation through latent code manipulation.

A significant shift occurred with the emergence of score-based modeling. To address the problem of ill-defined gradients when data resides on low-dimensional manifolds, arXiv:1907.05600 proposed estimating gradients of the data distribution via score matching on noise-perturbed data, utilizing annealed Langevin dynamics. This approach was further unified with diffusion probabilistic models (DPMs) in arXiv:2006.11239, which connected nonequilibrium thermodynamics to a weighted variational bound and denoising score matching. While DPMs proved highly effective for image synthesis, they were hindered by slow sampling speeds due to the requirement of simulating a Markov chain over many steps. This limitation was directly addressed by arXiv:2010.02502, which introduced non-Markovian diffusion processes (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models) to achieve sampling speeds 10x to 50x faster than standard DPMs using the same training procedure.

Modern generative capabilities have been expanded through multi-modal conditioning and latent space operations. The ability to bridge vision and language was advanced by arXiv:2103.00020, which utilized large-scale (image, text) pairs to enable zero-shot transfer, overcoming the limitations of fixed object categories. To combat the high computational costs of operating directly in pixel space, arXiv:2112.10752 proposed applying diffusion models within the latent space of pretrained autoencoders. By incorporating cross-attention layers, this method allows for flexible conditioning on text or bounding boxes, facilitating tasks ranging from semantic scene synthesis to super-resolution.

## Early Video Generation: GANs and Sequence Models

Before the dominance of diffusion-based architectures, early research in video generation focused on two distinct trajectories: sequence modeling for video-to-text tasks and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for visual synthesis. Initial efforts in video understanding utilized recurrent structures to bridge the gap between visual content and language. For instance, arXiv:1412.4729 addressed the scarcity of captioned video datasets by leveraging a unified deep neural network that transferred knowledge from over 1.2M category-labeled images to enable open-domain video description. While effective, subsequent work identified that local word generation often failed to capture holistic semantics. To address this, arXiv:1505.01861 introduced the LSTM-E framework, which jointly modeled visual-semantic embeddings and sentence generation to ensure better alignment between subjects, verbs, and objects.

Parallel to these linguistic models, the development of GANs for video synthesis focused on untangling temporal dynamics from spatial content. Early architectures like arXiv:1609.02612 utilized spatio-temporal convolutional structures to separate foreground from background for scene dynamics recognition. This concept of decoupling was further refined in the Temporal Generative Adversarial Nets (TGAN) proposed in arXiv:1611.06624, which employed a dual-generator architecture (temporal and image) and singular value clipping to mitigate the inherent instability of training complex GANs. Building on these foundations, arXiv:1707.07493 (MoCoGAN) achieved unsupervised decomposition of motion and content using both image and video discriminators.

As the field progressed, the primary challenge shifted from basic generation to the computational scaling required for high-resolution and long-duration videos. arXiv:1811.09245 addressed the exponential cost of resolution by using a stack of small sub-generators, allowing computational costs to scale linearly. Similarly, arXiv:1907.06571 introduced the Dual Video Discriminator GAN (DVD-GAN), which utilized a computationally efficient discriminator decomposition to handle complex datasets. 

A significant shift occurred when researchers moved away from discrete 3D RGB grids toward continuous representations. While arXiv:2112.14683 (StyleGAN-V) achieved continuous-time synthesis through positional embeddings and holistic temporal aggregation, arXiv:2202.10571 (DIGAN) addressed the limitations of grid-based scaling by utilizing Implicit Neural Representations (INRs). By manipulating differential space and time coordinates, DIGAN provided a more robust framework for modeling the spatio-temporal complexity and continuity required for long video generation. This evolution demonstrates a clear progression from simple recurrent sequence modeling to sophisticated adversarial frameworks capable of decomposing motion, scaling resolution, and eventually representing video as a continuous temporal signal.

## The Rise of Video Diffusion Models

The evolution of video generation has been fundamentally shaped by the transition from text-to-image diffusion frameworks to specialized spatio-temporal architectures. Early foundations were laid by GLIDE, which demonstrated the efficacy of text-conditional diffusion using CLIP and classifier-free guidance for image synthesis and inpainting (arXiv:2112.10741). Building upon these image-centric principles, researchers sought to extend diffusion to the temporal dimension. Video Diffusion Models (VDM) established a baseline by extending standard image architectures through conditional sampling and joint training on both image and video data to mitigate gradient variance (arXiv:2204.03458).

A significant challenge in this transition is the scarcity of paired text-video datasets and the immense computational costs associated with training large-scale models (arXiv:2205.15868). To circumvent the need for massive text-video datasets, Make-A-Video proposed decomposing temporal U-Net and attention tensors into spatial and temporal approximations, allowing the model to leverage paired text-image data alongside unsupervised video footage (arXiv:2209.14792). In contrast, CogVideo addressed the scale problem by utilizing a 9B-parameter transformer and a multi-frame-rate hierarchical training strategy, inheriting knowledge from a pretrained text-to-image model (arXiv:2205.15868). While Make-A-Video focuses on data efficiency, Imagen Video pursues high-definition output through a cascade of fully-convolutional spatial and temporal super-resolution models (arXiv:2210.02303).

Efficiency and temporal consistency remain central themes in recent developments. MagicVideo and Latent Video Diffusion Models (LVDM) both utilize low-dimensional latent spaces to reduce computational demands (arXiv:2211.13221; arXiv:2211.11018). However, they differ in their architectural approach: MagicVideo employs a 3D U-Net with a frame-wise lightweight adaptor and a directed temporal attention module (arXiv:2211.11018), whereas LVDM introduces hierarchical diffusion in a 3D latent space to support long-form video generation of over 1000 frames, using conditional latent perturbation to combat error accumulation (arXiv:2211.13221).

Recent research has also pivoted toward zero-shot capabilities and structural control. Text2Video-Zero bypasses the need for additional training entirely by reprogramming frame-level self-attention in existing text-to-image models (arXiv:2303.13439). For more controlled synthesis, Structure and Content-Guided Video Synthesis achieves disentanglement by training on monocular depth estimates (arXiv:2302.03011). Finally, large-scale implementations like ModelScope integrate spatio-temporal blocks within a 1.7 billion parameter denoising U-Net to ensure smooth movement and consistent frame generation (arXiv:2308.06571).

## Controllability and Motion-Aware Synthesis

Achieving high-fidelity video generation requires addressing the fundamental tension between maintaining spatial content integrity and introducing dynamic motion. Early advancements in spatial controllability were established by ControlNet, which utilizes zero-initialized convolution layers to inject spatial conditioning—such as depth or human pose—into pretrained text-to-image models without degrading existing features arXiv:2302.05543. This architectural principle of adding control without destroying learned weights serves as a foundation for several video-centric methodologies.

A primary challenge in video synthesis is the "intertwining" of spatial content and temporal dynamics, which increases modeling complexity arXiv:2312.04483. To mitigate this, HiGen proposes a hierarchical spatio-temporal decoupling paradigm, separating spatial reasoning from temporal reasoning through a unified denoiser arXiv:2312.04483. Similarly, AnimateDiff addresses the difficulty of adding motion to personalized text-to-image models by introducing a plug-and-play motion module and MotionLoRA, which learns transferable motion priors without requiring model-specific tuning arXiv:2307.04725. While AnimateDiff focuses on animating existing models, other approaches focus on extending latent diffusion models (LDMs) directly. For instance, Align your Latents extends pretrained image LDMs by introducing temporal layers to enable high-resolution synthesis arXiv:2304.08818, a strategy echoed by Stable Video Diffusion, which employs a three-stage training process—comprising text-to-image pretraining, video pretraining, and high-quality finetuning—to scale latent video diffusion to large datasets arXiv:2311.15127.

Beyond structural extensions, recent research emphasizes explicit motion guidance to improve temporal consistency. MoVideo addresses the failure of existing models to explicitly consider motion by incorporating depth and optical flow guidance in the latent space to regulate cross-frame correspondences arXiv:2311.11325. VideoComposer advances this compositional control by utilizing a Spatio-Temporal Condition (STC) encoder, which unifies spatial conditions (like sketches) with temporal conditions, such as motion vectors derived from compressed videos arXiv:2306.02018. This contrasts with FateZero, which focuses on zero-shot video editing rather than synthesis from scratch; FateZero utilizes attention fusion—blending self-attention and spatial-temporal attention maps—to ensure frame consistency during text-based editing without per-prompt training arXiv:2303.09535.

While these methods significantly improve controllability, they face varying degrees of complexity. For example, while the hierarchical decoupling in HiGen simplifies the reasoning process arXiv:2312.04483, the scaling strategies seen in Stable Video Diffusion require massive, systematically curated datasets to overcome the lack of unified training methods in the field arXiv:2311.15127. Collectively, the literature shows a transition from simple spatial conditioning arXiv:2302.05543 toward complex, decoupled, and motion-aware architectures that treat temporal dynamics as a distinct, controllable dimension arXiv:2306.02018, arXiv:2312.04483.

## Scaling, Efficiency, and Long-Term Consistency

The advancement of video diffusion models is currently constrained by high computational demands, data scarcity, and the difficulty of maintaining temporal coherence over extended durations. Recent research has addressed these challenges through architectural innovations, training-free refinement, and novel temporal modeling techniques.

Efficiency in video generation is primarily hindered by the quadratic complexity of attention layers and the high dimensionality of video data. To mitigate these costs, arXiv:2403.14148 proposes a Content-Frame Motion-Latent Decomposition (CMD) approach, which utilizes a compact latent space to separate content frames from low-dimensional motion latents, thereby allowing the integration of pretrained image diffusion models. Similarly, arXiv:2403.07711 seeks to overcome the quadratic scaling of attention by replacing it with linear-time bidirectional Structured State Spaces (SSMs), such as Mamba, to facilitate more efficient temporal feature extraction. Other frameworks attempt to reduce complexity by decoupling tasks; for instance, the ConFiner framework (arXiv:2408.13423) decomposes generation into structure control and spatial-temporal refinement using a chain of off-the-shelf diffusion experts.

Long-term consistency and temporal modeling present a separate set of challenges, particularly regarding the rigid sequence lengths of synchronous models and the error accumulation in asynchronous auto-regressive approaches. arXiv:2503.07418 introduces AR-Diffusion, which employs a non-decreasing corruption timestep constraint and specialized schedulers (FoPP and AD) to bridge the training-inference gap, enabling flexible video length generation. For scenarios where training is computationally prohibitive, arXiv:2503.09926 introduces VideoMerge, a training-free method that merges short videos from pretrained models to achieve extended durations. However, achieving smoothness and long-range consistency in such merged sequences remains a significant hurdle.

The precision of temporal modeling is further refined by addressing the limitations of scalar timestep variables. arXiv:2410.03160 introduces a Vectorized Timestep Approach (VTV), allowing each frame to follow an independent noise schedule to capture fine-grained temporal dependencies, which helps prevent catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning. In the realm of training-free guidance, arXiv:2501.07563 utilizes a motion consistency loss to capture inter-frame feature correlation patterns, providing gradient-based guidance to improve temporal coherence.

Finally, the progress in these areas must be measured against standardized metrics. While existing metrics often fail to align with human perception, the VBench suite (arXiv:2311.17982) provides a hierarchical, 16-dimension benchmark to dissect video quality across disentangled dimensions. This is critical as models attempt to overcome data limitations, such as the high-quality data scarcity addressed by VideoCrafter2 (arXiv:2401.09047), which finetunes spatial modules with high-quality images to shift distributions without degrading motion.

## The Current Frontier: Transformers and Advanced Video Diffusion

Recent advancements in video diffusion are increasingly defined by the transition toward Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) and the pursuit of efficient spatio-temporal modeling. A primary challenge in this transition is the entanglement of content and style features within DiT internal representations, which complicates tasks like style transfer [arXiv:2601.06202, arXiv:2601.20175]. To address this, researchers have proposed curriculum continual learning frameworks—training on mixtures of clean and noisy triplets to enable generalization to unseen styles without degrading content [arXiv:2601.06202, arXiv:2601.20175]. Building on this, VISTA introduces a specialized in-context framework using a lightweight style adapter and the VISTA-1000 synthetic dataset to mitigate temporal inconsistency and flickering artifacts often caused by long-term motion or occlusions [arXiv:2605.17312].

The architectural evolution of these models focuses heavily on the trade-off between computational cost and modeling depth. While Full 3D Attention provides comprehensive spatio-temporal modeling, it is prohibitively expensive, whereas Local Factorized Attention often fails to capture complex dynamics [arXiv:2603.09721]. FrameDiT attempts to bridge this gap by introducing Matrix Attention, which treats entire frames as matrices to enable efficient global spatio-temporal modeling [arXiv:2603.09721]. This drive for efficiency is echoed in broader surveys that categorize advancements into four paradigms: step distillation, efficient attention, model compression, and cache/trajectory optimization [arXiv:2604.15911].

Beyond architectural efficiency, recent methodologies focus on versatile control and high-fidelity synthesis. Temporal In-Context Fine-Tuning (TIC-FT) offers a way to achieve efficient adaptation for image-to-video and video-to-video tasks without requiring architectural changes, instead utilizing buffer frames of increasing noise levels to ensure smooth transitions [arXiv:2506.00996]. For temporal resolution, DiffuseSlide provides a training-free approach for high frame rate (FPS) generation by employing sliding window latent denoising and noise re-injection, specifically targeting the flickering and degradation common in long sequences [arXiv:2506.01454]. Furthermore, compositional synthesis has been advanced through object-centric learning, where pose-invariant slots are conditioned on pretrained models to allow for semantic editing, such as the insertion or deletion of objects [arXiv:2507.20855].

Collectively, these works highlight a shift from basic video generation toward highly controllable, efficient, and compositionally aware systems. However, significant hurdles remain, including the need for robust real-time long-horizon generation, the preservation of identity and motion coherence in image-to-video tasks [arXiv:2605.17248], and the ongoing struggle to balance computational efficiency with high-quality visual understanding [arXiv:2502.07001, arXiv:2504.16081].

## Tensions and Open Problems

A primary tension in the literature concerns the architectural foundation of video synthesis: whether models should prioritize global temporal coherence through unified diffusion or achieve high-fidelity motion through decoupled, object-centric control. Early large-scale approaches, such as those presented in arXiv:2210.02303, focus on high-definition diffusion to maintain visual quality, yet they often struggle with fine-grained compositional control. In contrast, arXiv:2507.20855 argues that such monolithic frameworks lack the necessary granularity for complex scenes, proposing instead a temporal object-centric learning approach to manage individual entity dynamics. This debate extends to the trade-off between simplicity and controllability; while arXiv:2209.14792 demonstrates that text-to-video can be achieved without massive text-video datasets, more recent critiques in arXiv:2302.07685 suggest that such methods may fail to capture the probabilistic complexities of latent space, necessitating more structured projection techniques to ensure temporal stability.

Another significant debate centers on the evaluation and benchmarking of generative quality. For years, the field relied on metrics derived from early adversarial frameworks, such as the convergence properties discussed in arXiv:1706.08500. However, recent scholarship suggests these metrics are insufficient for the nuances of video. Specifically, arXiv:2311.17982 critiques the reliance on older video metrics like those in arXiv:1812.01717, arguing that they fail to capture the holistic semantic and temporal dimensions required for modern generative models. This tension highlights a growing gap between the rapid advancement of model capabilities and the stagnant nature of the metrics used to validate them, creating an open problem regarding how to objectively quantify "motion realism" versus "visual fidelity."

Finally, there is an ongoing disagreement regarding the optimal path toward long-form video generation: whether to scale existing latent diffusion models or to move toward asynchronous, auto-regressive architectures. While models like those in arXiv:2311.15127 attempt to scale latent video diffusion to achieve stability, they are often criticized for computational inefficiency and a lack of structural decoupling, as noted in arXiv:2408.13423. This has led to a bifurcation in research, where some favor the massive scaling of unified transformers, while others advocate for the asynchronous, modular approaches seen in arXiv:2503.07418, which attempt to break the temporal dependency bottleneck.

## Outlook

The frontier of video generation is rapidly shifting from mere pixel synthesis toward sophisticated, content-preserving style transfer and efficient, high-frame-rate generation. As the field moves beyond the limitations of standard diffusion, the integration of Diffusion Transformers (DiT) with advanced attention mechanisms, such as the matrix attention explored in arXiv:2603.09721, promises to resolve the long-standing conflict between computational efficiency and temporal resolution. The next generation of models will likely move toward "asynchronous intelligence," where video is not generated as a single block, but as a continuous, evolving stream of temporally consistent and stylistically controllable representations.
