## Foundations of Generative Modeling and Early Diffusion

The evolution of generative modeling has transitioned from establishing fundamental variational frameworks to the development of sophisticated diffusion-based architectures. Early progress was anchored by the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) framework, which introduced the reparameterization of the variational lower bound to enable optimization via stochastic gradient methods and utilized a recognition model for efficient posterior inference in continuous latent variables [arXiv:1312.6114]. While VAEs provided a structured approach to directed probabilistic models, alternative paradigms emerged to capture data distributions through different mathematical lenses.

Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) introduced a minimax two-player game between a generator and a discriminator [arXiv:1406.2661]. This adversarial process was later refined to improve stability and resolution; for instance, progressive growing architectures allowed for incremental layer addition to model increasing levels of detail [arXiv:1710.10196], while style-based architectures enabled the unsupervised separation of high-level attributes and stochastic variation for improved disentanglement [arXiv:1812.04948].

A distinct lineage of modeling, rooted in non-equilibrium statistical physics, began with the proposal of a framework that systematically destroys data structure through a forward diffusion process to learn a tractable reverse process [arXiv:1503.03585]. This concept was significantly advanced by score-based generative models, which estimated gradients of the data distribution by perturbing data with varying levels of Gaussian noise and employing annealed Langevin dynamics [arXiv:1907.05600].

The convergence of these thermodynamic and score-based approaches culminated in the modern diffusion era. Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) established a connection between diffusion processes and denoising score matching [arXiv:2006.11239]. DDPMs demonstrated that diffusion models could serve as a viable alternative to GANs for high-quality image synthesis, achieving a state-of-the-art FID of 3.17 on CIFAR10 using $\epsilon$-prediction parameterization [arXiv:2006.11239]. However, despite this empirical success, the model's log-likelihoods remained uncompetitive compared to other likelihood-based generative models [arXiv:2006.11239].

This field was further unified by the introduction of a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) framework, which integrated score-based modeling and diffusion probabilistic modeling into a single continuous-time formalism [arXiv:2011.13456]. By introducing a predictor-corrector sampling framework and deriving an equivalent neural ODE for exact likelihood computation, this approach provided a more cohesive mathematical foundation [arXiv:2011.13456]. This unification allowed for superior performance across multiple metrics, including an Inception score of 9.89 and an FID of 2.20 [arXiv:2011.13456], effectively bridging the gap between discrete diffusion steps and continuous score-based estimation.

## Core Diffusion Architectures and Latent Representations

The evolution of diffusion architectures has transitioned from optimizing pixel-space denoising processes to sophisticated latent-space manipulations and transformer-based backbones. Early improvements focused on the mathematical efficiency of the reverse diffusion process; for instance, arXiv:2102.09672 enhanced Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) by learning the variances of the reverse process, enabling competitive log-likelihoods and significantly faster sampling through fewer forward passes. While these refinements optimized the sampling trajectory, they remained computationally intensive due to their operation in high-dimensional pixel space.

To address this complexity, a paradigm shift occurred with the introduction of Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs). arXiv:2112.10752 decoupled perceptual compression from semantic generation by applying diffusion processes within the latent space of pretrained autoencoders. This approach introduced cross-attention layers for flexible multi-modal conditioning, offering a scalable framework for high-resolution synthesis. However, this efficiency comes with a trade-off: the model exhibits high sensitivity to the downsampling factor $f$, where excessive compression can lead to information loss and stagnating fidelity. Subsequent research has sought to scale this latent approach. arXiv:2307.01952 improved upon the LDM framework by tripling the U-Net backbone size, utilizing dual text encoders, and introducing a post-hoc refinement model to enhance visual fidelity for high-resolution synthesis.

Architectural diversity has also expanded through hierarchical and backbone-replacement strategies. arXiv:2106.15282 proposed a cascaded pipeline of multiple diffusion models (low-resolution followed by super-resolution models) to achieve high fidelity, utilizing conditioning augmentation to prevent compounding errors. In contrast, arXiv:2204.06125 adopted a two-stage hierarchical approach that explicitly generates CLIP image embeddings as an intermediate step, thereby improving image diversity and semantic variation. Moving away from the traditional U-Net, arXiv:2212.09748 introduced Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), which replace the U-Net backbone with a transformer architecture operating on latent patches, demonstrating clear scalability via forward pass complexity.

The integration of language models has further refined text-to-image capabilities. While arXiv:2204.06125 relies on CLIP latents, arXiv:2205.11487 demonstrated that scaling large pre-trained text-only language models (such as T5) improves sample fidelity and image-text alignment more effectively than scaling the image diffusion model itself. Finally, the fundamental design space of these models has been clarified by arXiv:2206.00364, which separates design choices across sampling, training, and score network preconditioning, providing a framework to optimize the balance between network evaluations and generative performance.

## Acceleration, Distillation, and Efficient Sampling

The computational burden of diffusion models stems from the iterative nature of their reverse processes, necessitating significant research into acceleration techniques. Early advancements focused on modifying the underlying stochastic process; for instance, Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIMs) introduced non-Markovian diffusion processes that maintain the same training objective as DDPMs but allow for much faster sampling, achieving 10x to 50x speedups in wall-clock time [arXiv:2010.02502]. While DDIMs provide a foundation for faster sampling through non-Markovian trajectories [arXiv:2010.02502], subsequent work shifted toward treating the sampling process as an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) to leverage advanced numerical solvers.

A significant leap in efficiency is found in high-order ODE solvers. DPM-Solver provides an exact formulation of the diffusion ODE solution, utilizing an exponentially weighted integral of the neural network to achieve convergence in approximately 10 steps, with reported FID scores of 4.70 at 10 steps and 2.87 at 20 steps on CIFAR10 [arXiv:2206.00927]. This approach is further refined by DPM-Solver++, which introduces a multistep variant to address instability and incorporates thresholding methods to maintain data distribution matching, specifically targeting guided sampling for high-resolution synthesis in both pixel and latent spaces [arXiv:2211.01095].

While ODE solvers optimize the sampling trajectory of an existing model, distillation methods aim to fundamentally reduce the number of required steps by training a student model to mimic a teacher. Progressive Distillation offers a method to halve the required sampling steps at each iteration, capable of reducing steps from 8192 down to just 4, achieving an FID of 3.0 on CIFAR-10 [arXiv:2202.00512]. However, more recent distillation frameworks have moved toward even more aggressive reductions for real-time synthesis. Adversarial Diffusion Distillation (ADD) combines score distillation with an adversarial loss to enable high-fidelity synthesis in as few as 1–4 steps [arXiv:2311.17042]. Building upon these concepts, Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD2) improves upon previous distillation methods by eliminating regression loss through a two-time-scale update rule for the critic and integrating a GAN loss using real data [arXiv:2405.14867]. This mitigates the issue of imperfect teacher score estimation, resulting in highly efficient one-step synthesis with an FID of 1.28 on ImageNet-64x64 [arXiv:2405.14867].

In summary, the literature reveals a progression from structural modifications of the diffusion process [arXiv:2010.02502] to sophisticated ODE-based numerical solvers [arXiv:2206.00927, arXiv:2211.01095], and finally to advanced distillation techniques [arXiv:2202.00512, arXiv:2311.17042, arXiv:2405.14867] that trade training complexity for near-instantaneous inference.

## Controllable Generation and Image-to-Image Tasks

The evolution of diffusion models has transitioned from general image synthesis toward highly controllable frameworks capable of precise image-to-image translation and subject-specific personalization. Early approaches to image manipulation, such as SDEdit, utilized Stochastic Differential Equations (SDE) to synthesize or edit images by adding noise to an input and subsequently denoising it through an SDE prior [arXiv:2108.01073]. This method is notable for requiring no task-specific training or complex inversions, achieving up to a 98.09% improvement in realism and 91.72% in satisfaction according to human perception studies [arXiv:2108.01073]. While SDEdit provides a flexible generative prior, other works have sought more unified frameworks for specific translation tasks. For instance, Palette introduces a conditional diffusion model framework that handles diverse image-to-image tasks—including colorization, inpainting, and JPEG restoration—performing as well as or better than task-specific specialists without requiring auxiliary losses or specialized tuning [arXiv:2111.05826].

As the field progressed, control shifted from global image manipulation to fine-grained spatial and semantic conditioning. ControlNet represents a significant architectural advancement by introducing "zero convolutions" to connect a locked, pretrained diffusion backbone to new spatial conditioning layers [arXiv:2302.05543]. Unlike the stochastic noise-addition approach of SDEdit [arXiv:2108.01073], ControlNet allows for precise structural guidance through edges, depth maps, or human poses, effectively preventing the introduction of harmful noise during the fine-tuning process [arXiv:2302.05543]. Parallel to this structural control, DreamBooth addresses semantic control by fine-tuning pretrained text-to-image models to bind unique identifiers to specific subjects [arXiv:2208.12242]. By employing an autogenous class-specific prior preservation loss, DreamBooth enables subject recontextualization and view synthesis, moving beyond the general image synthesis seen in earlier models [arXiv:2208.12242].

The underlying mechanics of these generative trajectories have also been scrutinized. While models like Palette [arXiv:2111.05826] and ControlNet [arXiv:2302.05543] focus on task execution, analytical work has sought to explain the "outline first, details later" phenomenon observed in pretrained models [arXiv:2303.02490]. By deriving a closed-form solution to the probability flow ODE for Gaussian distributions, researchers have provided a mathematical basis for how these models traverse the image manifold during the reverse diffusion process [arXiv:2303.02490]. This theoretical understanding complements broader surveys that categorize the landscape of diffusion techniques, including Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) and score-based modeling, while highlighting the importance of optimization strategies like Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) and noise scheduling in enhancing high-quality generation [arXiv:2402.16369, arXiv:2412.14422].

## Expansion to Video, 3D, and Multi-modal Domains

The transition from static image generation to temporal and volumetric domains has necessitated significant architectural and training paradigm shifts. In the video domain, early efforts focused on extending standard image diffusion architectures to enable joint training from image and video data, utilizing new conditional sampling techniques for spatial and temporal extension [arXiv:2204.03458]. However, generating high-fidelity, long-duration sequences remains a challenge due to error accumulation. Latent Video Diffusion Models (LVDM) address this by operating within a low-dimensional 3D latent space, employing hierarchical diffusion and conditional latent perturbation with unconditional guidance to maintain consistency across sequences exceeding 1000 frames [arXiv:2211.13221]. This latent-space approach is further scaled in Stable Video Diffusion, which identifies a critical three-stage training strategy—comprising text-to-image pretraining, video pretraining, and high-quality video finetuning—to achieve high-resolution text-to-video and image-to-video capabilities [arXiv:2311.15127]. While some models rely on synthesizing keyframes followed by temporal super-resolution, Lumiere introduces a Space-Time U-Net architecture that generates the entire temporal duration in a single pass through multiple space-time scales, offering a more integrated approach to video generation [arXiv:2401.12945].

The expansion into 3D content creation leverages diffusion models as priors to overcome the scarcity of 3D data. One approach involves lifting pretrained 2D models via score Jacobian chaining, applying the chain rule through a differentiable renderer to back-propagate 2D scores into 3D space [arXiv:2212.00774]. To improve high-resolution results, Magic3D utilizes a two-stage optimization framework: a low-resolution diffusion prior followed by a high-resolution latent diffusion model using a sparse 3D hash grid and a textured 3D mesh [arXiv:2211.10440]. This method demonstrated significant efficiency gains, reducing generation time from 1.5 hours to 40 minutes and achieving a 61.7% user preference over previous methods [arXiv:2211.10440]. 

Recent advancements have moved toward multi-view and zero-shot capabilities. Zero-1-to-3 enables novel view synthesis from a single RGB image by leveraging geometric priors learned from large-scale diffusion models [arXiv:2303.11328]. Building on these concepts, MVDream introduces a multi-view diffusion model that learns from both 2D and 3D data to serve as a generalizable 3D prior, facilitating consistent multi-view image generation [arXiv:2308.16512]. While these models provide robust 3D priors, they are subject to critique regarding their consistency and representation agnosticism [arXiv:2309.16653]. Collectively, these developments illustrate a trajectory from simple temporal extensions to complex, multi-scale, and multi-view frameworks that bridge the gap between 2D pixels and 3D geometry.

## The Frontier: Diffusion Language Models and Advanced Manifold Modeling

Recent advancements have shifted the focus of diffusion modeling from pure image synthesis toward the complex discrete structures of natural language and the explicit geometric modeling of data manifolds. A primary tension in this frontier involves the methodology for transitioning from established autoregressive (AR) paradigms to diffusion-based generative modeling.

One approach involves adapting existing AR architectures through continual pre-training. For instance, arXiv:2410.17891 introduces DiffuGPT and DiffuLLaMA, which adapt GPT2 and LLaMA into diffusion models to handle tasks such as reasoning and instruction following. This strategy is further refined by arXiv:2508.15487, which introduces Dream 7B; this model extends the adaptation approach of arXiv:2410.17891 by incorporating context-adaptive token-level noise rescheduling, allowing for tunable quality-speed trade-offs and arbitrary-order generation. In contrast to these adaptation-based methods, arXiv:2502.09992 proposes LLaDA, a large-scale diffusion model trained from scratch using pre-training and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) paradigms. LLaDA explicitly challenges the necessity of AR models for achieving LLM-level capabilities in math, code, and in-context learning.

The mathematical formulation of these language models varies significantly. While arXiv:2406.07524 utilizes a simplified, Rao-Blackwellized objective that functions as a mixture of classical masked language modeling losses for efficient semi-autoregressive generation, arXiv:2502.09992 employs a forward data masking process and a reverse generation process that optimizes a likelihood lower bound. Moving toward higher efficiency, arXiv:2505.22618 addresses the inference bottleneck of bidirectional diffusion LLMs like LLaDA by introducing a block-wise approximate KV Cache and confidence-aware parallel decoding, achieving up to a 27.6x improvement in throughput. Furthering the complexity of the modeling space, arXiv:2605.07933 moves beyond discrete token modeling to propose Latent Diffusion Language Models (LDLM), which perform continuous latent space diffusion on reshaped representations of pre-trained models through a joint training recipe involving adaptive timestep sampling.

Parallel to these linguistic developments, researchers are improving the fidelity of continuous manifold modeling. arXiv:2606.00094 introduces the MIND model, which integrates discrete patch tokenization directly into the score function of a continuous diffusion model. By utilizing a soft top-k aggregation mechanism and dual-branch high-frequency feature embedding, it explicitly models manifold geometry to improve image generation. This focus on sampling flexibility is echoed in arXiv:2506.01541, which proposes decoupling generation and destruction variances. By treating both transition kernels as unconstrained Gaussian densities, this method allows for trainable destruction processes optimized for extremely few generation steps, offering a more flexible framework for sampling unnormalized densities.

## Tensions and Open Problems

A primary tension in the field concerns the fundamental trade-off between sampling speed and distributional fidelity. While early distillation techniques, such as those proposed in arXiv:2210.03142, focused on distilling guided diffusion models to accelerate inference, they often struggled with the precision of the generated samples. This gap has led to a divergence in methodology: some researchers advocate for more robust distribution matching approaches, as seen in arXiv:2405.14867, to ensure that fast sampling does not come at the cost of mode collapse or reduced quality. Similarly, the evolution of consistency-based modeling highlights a structural disagreement; while arXiv:2303.01469 introduced consistency models to enable single-step generation, arXiv:2310.02279 argues that these models must be refined into Consistency Trajectory Models to better align with the underlying probability flow ODEs, suggesting that the initial pursuit of speed may have bypassed necessary mathematical rigor in trajectory modeling.

Another significant debate revolves around the architectural representation of complex, high-dimensional data, particularly in the transition from 2D to 3D generation. There is a growing tension between multi-view consistency and explicit geometric reconstruction. For instance, arXiv:2308.16512 emphasizes the importance of multi-view diffusion to maintain structural coherence across different perspectives, yet arXiv:2309.16653 critiques this approach by demonstrating that generative Gaussian Splatting can achieve superior efficiency and 3D fidelity without the heavy computational overhead of multi-view diffusion. This reflects a broader uncertainty in the community regarding whether generative models should prioritize the learning of implicit neural representations or focus on the explicit synthesis of textured primitives to achieve photorealism.

Finally, a conflict persists regarding the optimal training objectives for large-scale generative models. The community is split between those who favor maximizing likelihood through score-matching frameworks and those who argue for the necessity of adversarial or guided constraints to ensure high-frequency detail. While arXiv:2101.09258 focuses on the stability of maximum likelihood training for score-based models, arXiv:2112.07804 suggests that such approaches must be carefully balanced to solve the "generative learning trilemma"—the simultaneous requirement for sample quality, mode coverage, and fast sampling. This tension is further complicated by the emergence of latent-space manipulation, where the focus shifts from raw pixel-space modeling to the joint optimization of language and latent manifolds, as explored in arXiv:2605.07933.

## Outlook

The frontier of generative modeling is rapidly shifting from static image synthesis toward the mastery of temporal dynamics and manifold geometry. As we move beyond simple denoising, the field is heading toward a unified understanding of the "diffusion-attention connection" (arXiv:2604.09560) and the integration of explicit data manifold geometry (arXiv:2606.00094) to ensure structural integrity. The next generation of models will likely transcend current limitations by treating generation not merely as a reversal of destruction, but as a sophisticated process of exploration (arXiv:2605.30553), moving toward highly efficient, video-centric architectures that can seamlessly bridge the gap between latent representations and continuous physical reality.
