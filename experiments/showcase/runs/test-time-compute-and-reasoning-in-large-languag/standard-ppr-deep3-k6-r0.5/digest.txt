DEEP CRITIQUES (full-text peer review of key papers):
arXiv:2408.03314:
NOVELTY: A "compute-optimal" scaling strategy that adaptively allocates test-time compute (iterative revisions vs. PRM-based search) based on predicted prompt difficulty.
STRENGTHS: Demonstrates that compute-optimal scaling can outperform Best-of-N with 4× less compute and can outperform a 14× larger model in FLOPs-matched evaluations on easy/medium problems.
WEAKNESSES: The efficiency gains do not account for the additional inference
arXiv:2501.12948:
NOVELTY: Proposes a pure RL framework (DeepSeek-R1-Zero) that bypasses supervised fine-tuning to allow the emergence of non-human-like reasoning patterns (self-reflection, verification) via rule-based rewards.
STRENGTHS: Demonstrates massive performance jumps in verifiable domains, specifically increasing AIME 2024 accuracy from 15.6% to 77.9% through autonomous self-evolution.
WEAKNESSES: Exhibits token inefficiency ("overthinking") on simple tasks and faces scalability limits in non-verifiable domains (e.g., writing) where reliable rule-based rewards are difficult to construct.
RELEVANCE: Directly advances the study of test-time compute by demonstrating how RL can incentivize models to dynamically allocate more reasoning tokens and "thinking time" to complex problems.
arXiv:2505.20522:
NOVELTY: Derives a unified mathematical saturation point ($N^*$) for both parallel and sequential test-time scaling paradigms using a probabilistic model (TTSPM).

STRENGTHS: Demonstrates strong empirical correlation between theoretical predictions and observed scaling plateaus, specifically achieving Pearson $r = 0.803$ for the 7B model under parallel scaling.

WEAKNESSES: The model relies on the highly simplifying assumption of conditional independence between scaling units, which likely fails in sequential scaling due to error propagation and context dependency.

RELEVANCE: Provides a principled framework for determining the optimal computational budget for test-time reasoning, addressing the efficiency limits of "scaling over scaling."
arXiv:2512.02008:
NOVELTY: Establishes a model-aware taxonomy (short-horizon vs. long-horizon) to explain why universal test-time scaling (TTS) strategies fail, providing a decision matrix based on post-training origins.

STRENGTHS: Large-scale empirical validation across 8 models (7B–235B) and 4 datasets, demonstrating that beam search often exhibits inverse-scaling (performance degradation as $N$ increases).

WEAKNESSES: The "recipe" relies on the assumption that post-training algorithms (e.g., GRPO vs. GSPO) are the primary drivers of reasoning horizons, a correlation that lacks direct causal proof in the text.

RELEVANCE: Moves the literature from "more compute is better" to a nuanced understanding of how to allocate compute optimally based on model type and budget.
arXiv:2605.10344:
NOVELTY: Introduction of hierarchical memory (Experience vs. Guideline banks) and a hybrid reward RL scheme that explicitly incentivizes experience utilization and strategy novelty.
STRENGTHS: Significant performance gains on IMO-AnswerBench, specifically increasing Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 Pass@1 from 53.5% to 73.5%.
WEAKNESSES: Heavy reliance on a "cold-start" dataset distilled from a teacher model (DeepSeek-V3.2) and high computational overhead from running multiple specialized agents and verification passes across many iterations.
RELEVANCE: Advances the review by demonstrating how structured multi-agent coordination and memory management can optimize the efficiency of test-time compute scaling.
arXiv:2201.11903:
NOVELTY: Introduces "chain-of-thought prompting," a method to elicit multi-step reasoning by providing ⟨input, chain of thought, output⟩ triples in few-shot exemplars, rather than relying on fine-tuning or formal symbolic languages.

STRENGTHS: Demonstrates that CoT is an emergent property of scale, showing that PaLM 540B achieves new state-of-the-art accuracy on the GSM8K benchmark, significantly outperforming standard prompting.

WEAKNESSES: The method's efficacy is strictly tied to large model scales (~100B+ parameters), as smaller models produce illogical reasoning paths, and the paper acknowledges that the "chain" does not guarantee a correct reasoning path or factual accuracy.

RELEVANCE: Establishes a foundational mechanism for increasing test-time compute by allowing models to allocate more intermediate tokens (reasoning steps) to complex problems, directly addressing the limits of standard prompting in reasoning tasks.
