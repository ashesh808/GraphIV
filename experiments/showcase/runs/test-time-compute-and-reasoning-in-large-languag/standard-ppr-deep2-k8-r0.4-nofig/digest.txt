DEEP CRITIQUES (full-text peer review of key papers):
arXiv:2110.14168:
NOVELTY: Introduces GSM8K, a high-diversity grade school math dataset, and a verification framework that uses a separate model to rank multiple sampled completions.

STRENGTHS: Demonstrates that verification scales more efficiently with data than fine-tuning, providing a performance boost equivalent to a 30x increase in model size.

WEAKNESSES: Test-time compute benefits are non-monotonic; increasing the number of completions beyond 400 leads to performance degradation due to the risk of finding adversarial solutions that fool the verifier.

RELEVANCE: Establishes a foundational method for improving reasoning via test-time search and ranking, directly addressing the limitations of autoregressive generation in multi-step tasks.
arXiv:2203.11171:
NOVELTY: Introduces an unsupervised "sample-and-marginalize" decoding strategy that uses a single model to "self-ensemble" diverse reasoning paths.
STRENGTHS: Demonstrates significant accuracy gains across multiple scales and benchmarks, notably +17.9% on GSM8K and +12.2% on AQuA.
WEAKNESSES: Increases computational cost per query and is currently limited to tasks with a fixed answer set.
RELEVANCE: Provides a direct mechanism for improving reasoning reliability by trading increased test-time compute (sampling) for higher accuracy.
arXiv:2501.12948:
NOVELTY: Proposes a pure RL framework (DeepSeek-R1-Zero) that bypasses supervised fine-tuning to allow the emergence of non-human-like reasoning patterns (self-reflection, verification) via rule-based rewards.
STRENGTHS: Demonstrates massive performance jumps in verifiable domains, specifically increasing AIME 2024 accuracy from 15.6% to 77.9% through autonomous self-evolution.
WEAKNESSES: Exhibits token inefficiency ("overthinking") on simple tasks and faces scalability limits in non-verifiable domains (e.g., writing) where reliable rule-based rewards are difficult to construct.
RELEVANCE: Directly advances the study of test-time compute by demonstrating how RL can incentivize models to dynamically allocate more reasoning tokens and "thinking time" to complex problems.
arXiv:2512.02008:
NOVELTY: Establishes a model-aware taxonomy (short-horizon vs. long-horizon) to explain why universal test-time scaling (TTS) strategies fail, providing a decision matrix based on post-training origins.

STRENGTHS: Large-scale empirical validation across 8 models (7B–235B) and 4 datasets, demonstrating that beam search often exhibits inverse-scaling (performance degradation as $N$ increases).

arXiv:2603.07159:
NOVELTY: Shifts uncertainty-driven selection from token-level or full-trajectory sampling to the granularity of "thoughts" (intermediate reasoning steps).
STRENGTHS: Demonstrates that self-certainty maximization matches or exceeds self-consistency under equivalent token budgets and that optimizing only the first 1–5 steps yields peak accuracy.
WEAKNESSES: The method's reliance on predefined reasoning delimiters (e.g., double line breaks) to segment thoughts may not generalize to models without structured CoT formatting.
RELEVANCE: Advances test-time compute research by identifying an efficient, model-internal scaling strategy that prioritizes early-stage reasoning decisions.
arXiv:2603.21301:
NOVELTY: A systematic comparative evaluation of three distinct inference-time strategies—stochastic self-consistency, dual-model agreement, and self-reflection—under identical prompting and verification settings.
STRENGTHS: Self-consistency using controlled stochastic decoding ($T=0.8, p=0.9$) demonstrated a significant 9%–15% absolute accuracy improvement over greedy single-pass decoding.
WEAKNESSES: Dual-model reasoning actually decreased accuracy (47.4% vs. 48.7% baseline), and self-reflection yielded only marginal gains (3.4%), suggesting these methods may be ineffective for the smaller, non-reasoning models tested.
RELEVANCE: Benchmarks different methods of allocating test-time compute (sampling, multi-model verification, and iterative refinement) to improve reasoning reliability without parameter updates.
arXiv:2201.11903:
NOVELTY: Introduces "chain-of-thought prompting," a method to elicit multi-step reasoning by providing intermediate natural language steps in few-shot exemplars rather than just input-output pairs.

STRENGTHS: Demonstrates that CoT is an emergent property of scale, with PaLM 540B achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on the GSM8K benchmark, significantly outperforming standard prompting.

WEAKNESSES: The method relies on manual construction of reasoning exemplars and does not guarantee the logical correctness of the generated paths, potentially leading to "correct" answers via flawed reasoning.

RELEVANCE: Directly addresses the review question by showing how increasing test-time compute (via generating intermediate tokens) can unlock reasoning capabilities in large-scale models.
arXiv:2506.12928:
NOVELTY: First systematic exploration of test-time scaling (TTS) specifically for language agents, moving beyond end-to-end LLM reasoning to multi-step agentic frameworks.
STRENGTHS: Demonstrates that Best-of-N (BoN) achieves significant performance gains (8 points over baseline) on GAIA Level 1 and 2, and identifies that list-wise verification/merging outperforms scoring and voting.
WEAKNESSES: The claim that "knowing when to reflect is important" is poorly quantified; the study shows reflection can actually decrease performance (55.15 vs 55.76 baseline) and relies on arbitrary score thresholds (<8, <5, <2) without explaining their derivation.
RELEVANCE: Provides a taxonomy of agentic TTS strategies (parallel sampling, sequential revision, verifiers, and rollout diversity) to guide the transition from single-turn reasoning to multi-step agentic execution.
