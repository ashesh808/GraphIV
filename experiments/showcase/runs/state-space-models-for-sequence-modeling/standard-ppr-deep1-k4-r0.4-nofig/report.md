# From Recurrence to Selection: The Evolution and Rise of State Space Models in Sequence Modeling

The fundamental challenge of sequence modeling lies in the inherent tension between computational efficiency and the ability to capture long-range dependencies. For decades, the field was defined by the foundations of sequence modeling and recurrence, where recurrent architectures attempted to compress history into a fixed-size hidden state. While these methods were computationally efficient, they often struggled with the vanishing gradient problem and the inability to maintain context over vast distances. The subsequent Transformer era addressed these limitations through efficient attention mechanisms, enabling unprecedented scaling and performance. However, this success introduced a new bottleneck: the quadratic complexity of self-attention, which renders the processing of extremely long sequences computationally prohibitive and memory-intensive.

The limitations of the attention mechanism have catalyzed a resurgence of interest in state space models (SSMs) as a scalable alternative. This trajectory began with the emergence of structured state space models, which leverage linear time complexity to bridge the gap between continuous dynamical systems and discrete sequence processing. The field has recently undergone a transformative shift known as the selective SSM revolution, where models like Mamba have introduced input-dependent selection mechanisms to overcome the limitations of previous time-invariant architectures. By allowing the model to selectively filter information, these new paradigms achieve a level of expressivity that rivals Transformers while maintaining the efficiency of recurrent systems.

This review traces the historical and mathematical arc of these developments, beginning with the theoretical frameworks and duality that connect recurrence, convolution, and state space representations. We then explore the transition from structured, time-invariant models to the recent breakthroughs in selective architectures. The discussion further extends to the practical deployment of these models through specialized architectures and domain applications, ultimately concluding with an analysis of hardware acceleration and the future frontiers that will determine the next generation of sequence modeling technology.

## Foundations of Sequence Modeling and Recurrence

The foundation of sequence modeling is rooted in managing the dynamical complexities of recurrent architectures. A primary obstacle in this domain is the instability of training caused by vanishing and exploding gradients [arXiv:1211.5063]. To address these, researchers proposed gradient norm clipping and soft constraints to stabilize the learning process [arXiv:1211.5063].

Architectural advancements have since focused on refining these recurrent structures through gating mechanisms. While traditional tanh-based RNNs face inherent limitations, gated recurrent units, such as LSTMs and GRUs, offer superior empirical performance in modeling complex signals like speech and polyphonic music [arXiv:1412.3555]. These gated architectures can be further integrated into more complex frameworks, such as the RNN Encoder-Decoder, which employs two recurrent networks to maximize the conditional probability of a target sequence given a source sequence for statistical machine translation [arXiv:1406.1078].

However, a fundamental tension exists between the sequential nature of these recurrent models and the demand for computational efficiency. Traditional RNNs are inherently difficult to parallelize, a limitation that Quasi-Recurrent Neural Networks (QRNNs) seek to overcome [arXiv:1611.01576]. By substituting trainable recurrent layers with parallel convolutional layers and applying channel-wise recurrent pooling, QRNNs can achieve up to 16 times faster training and testing speeds compared to LSTMs [arXiv:1611.01576]. A similar shift toward parallelization is seen in gated convolutional networks, which use stacked convolutions and a simplified gating mechanism to allow parallelization over sequential tokens [arXiv:1612.08083]. While these convolutional models significantly reduce sentence scoring latency compared to recurrent baselines, they are fundamentally constrained by a finite context approach, even though they remain capable of capturing long-term dependencies [arXiv:1612.08083].

Crucial to the training of all such large-scale sequence models is the optimization strategy employed. The Adam optimizer [arXiv:1412.6980] has become a standard due to its ability to use adaptive estimates of first and second moments, providing an efficient, low-memory solution for stochastic optimization, particularly when dealing with non-stationary objectives or sparse gradients [arXiv:1412.6980].

## The Transformer Era and Efficient Attention Mechanisms

The introduction of the Transformer architecture marked a paradigm shift in sequence modeling by dispensing with recurrence and convolutions in favor of multi-head self-attention to capture global dependencies [arXiv:1706.03762]. This architecture enabled significantly higher parallelization and reduced training times compared to its predecessors [arXiv:1706.03762]. Subsequent developments leveraged this foundation to enhance bidirectional context modeling, most notably with BERT, which utilizes a Transformer encoder to jointly condition on both left and right context for various NLP tasks [arXiv:1810.04805].

Despite its success, the standard Transformer suffers from quadratic time and memory complexity relative to sequence length [arXiv:1904.10509]. This bottleneck has driven a diverse body of research focused on efficient attention mechanisms. One approach involves structural modifications to the attention matrix; for instance, Sparse Transformers utilize sparse factorizations to reduce complexity to $O(n \sqrt{n})$ [arXiv:1904.10509], while Transformer-XL introduces segment-level recurrence and relative positional encoding to extend context beyond fixed lengths, achieving up to 1,800x faster evaluation [arXiv:1901.02860]. Other models, such as Reformer, employ locality-sensitive hashing (LSH) and reversible residual layers to reduce complexity to $O(L \log L)$ and minimize memory usage [arXiv:2001.04451]. Similarly, Longformer achieves linear scaling by combining local windowed attention with task-motivated global attention [arXiv:2004.05150].

A parallel line of inquiry seeks to achieve linear complexity through mathematical approximations of the attention mechanism. Linformer utilizes low-rank matrix approximation to reduce complexity to $O(n)$ [arXiv:2006.04768], while Performer employs the FAVOR+ approach, using positive Orthogonal Random features to provide an unbiased estimation of the softmax attention-kernel [arXiv:2009.14794]. Moving beyond approximation, "Transformers are RNNs" demonstrates that by applying the associativity property of matrix products to kernel feature maps, self-attention can be expressed as a linear dot-product, enabling an iterative RNN-like implementation that is up to 4000x faster on very long sequences [arXiv:2006.16236].

While many of these methods focus on approximating the attention matrix to improve efficiency, FlashAttention offers a distinct hardware-aware alternative. Rather than approximating the mechanism, FlashAttention implements exact attention by using IO-aware tiling to minimize memory reads/writes between GPU HBM and SRAM [arXiv:2205.14135]. This optimization of IO complexity allows for faster wall-clock speeds—such as a 3x speedup on GPT-2—and enables the processing of much longer sequences (e.g., 64K tokens in Path-256) without sacrificing the mathematical exactness of the original Transformer [arXiv:2205.14135].

## Emergence of Structured State Space Models

The evolution of state space models (SSMs) has transitioned from theoretical frameworks for optimal memory to highly efficient, structured architectures capable of surpassing Transformers in long-range dependency tasks. This progression began with the HiPPO framework, which introduced a recurrent memory mechanism using polynomial projections (specifically Legendre Memory Units) to achieve optimal online function approximation [arXiv:2008.07669]. By projecting continuous signals onto polynomial bases, HiPPO provides a method for compressing history with bounded gradients and timescale robustness [arXiv:2008.07669].

Building upon these foundations, the Structured State Space (S4) model introduced a Normal Plus Low-Rank (NPLR) parameterization of the state matrix $A$ [arXiv:2111.00396]. This innovation enables stable diagonalization and efficient computation via a Cauchy kernel, allowing S4 to solve the 16k-length Path-X task with 88% accuracy—a feat where prior models failed [arXiv:2111.00396]. While S4 unifies continuous, recurrent, and convolutional representations into an $O(N+L)$ complexity framework [arXiv:2111.00396], it faces potential numerical instability if $A$ has eigenvalues in the right half-plane [arXiv:2111.00396]. Furthermore, research suggests that S4's performance may be driven more by HiPPO initialization than the NPLR parameterization itself [arXiv:2111.00396].

Subsequent research focused on simplifying these structures. The Linear State-Space Layer (LSSL) generalized RNNs and temporal convolutions by simulating linear continuous-time equations [arXiv:2110.13985]. This led to the discovery that diagonal state matrices alone can match S4's performance, eliminating the need for complex low-rank corrections [arXiv:2203.14343]. This was further refined by the S4D model, which provides a systematic parameterization and initialization of diagonal SSMs that recovers the S4 kernel in the limit of infinite state dimension [arXiv:2206.11893], offering a significantly simpler implementation [arXiv:2206.11893].

Efficiency and hardware utilization became central themes in later iterations. The S5 model replaced multiple independent single-input, single-output SSMs with a single multi-input, multi-output (MIMO) architecture, leveraging parallel scans to match S4's efficiency while achieving state-of-the-art results on the Long Range Arena [arXiv:2208.04933]. For language modeling, the H3 architecture introduced a fused block FFT algorithm (FlashConv) to improve hardware utilization, enabling text generation 2.4x faster than Transformers [arXiv:2212.14052]. More recently, the field has explored theoretical reframing, such as viewing SSMs as deep Wiener models [arXiv:2312.06211] or utilizing spectral filtering algorithms to provide provable robustness regardless of the underlying dynamics' spectrum [arXiv:2312.06837].

## The Selective SSM Revolution: Mamba and Beyond

The landscape of sequence modeling has undergone a significant shift toward State Space Models (SSMs) that attempt to reconcile the parallelizable training of Transformers with the efficient, constant-complexity inference of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs). A foundational approach in this transition is RWKV (Receptance Weighted Key Value), which utilizes a linear attention mechanism to achieve a dual formulation: it can be trained in parallel like a Transformer but operates with constant computational and memory complexity during inference, effectively bypassing the quadratic scaling bottleneck of standard attention [arXiv:2305.13048].

While RWKV provides a bridge between these paradigms, recent advancements have focused on making SSMs more "selective" to better handle complex information filtering. Mamba introduces a Selective SSM where parameters are functions of the input, enabling the model to selectively propagate or forget information [arXiv:2312.00752]. This input-dependency allows Mamba to achieve 5x higher throughput than Transformers and scale linearly with sequence length, remaining effective on real data up to million-length sequences [arXiv:2312.00752]. This concept of selectivity is further refined in S7, which utilizes stable reparameterization to enable input-dependent filtering while maintaining gradient stability to prevent exploding or vanishing gradients [arXiv:2410.03464]. The theoretical capability of these models is underscored by proofs demonstrating that SSMs combined with nonlinear layers are provably comparable to Transformers in dynamic token selection and function estimation [arXiv:2405.19036].

Different architectural strategies have emerged to target specific data modalities. For time series, a companion matrix parameterization allows SSMs to express autoregressive processes, with a "closed-loop" variation enabling long-horizon forecasting [arXiv:2303.09489]. In contrast, for spatiotemporal modeling (e.g., Minecraft or Moving-MNIST), ConvSSM (specifically ConvS5) combines the tensor modeling strengths of ConvLSTM with the long-sequence capabilities of SSMs via parallel scans, generating samples 400x faster than Transformers [arXiv:2310.19694]. 

Rather than replacing Transformers entirely, some researchers propose hybridizing these strengths. The Block-State Transformer (BST) integrates SSM sublayers for long-range contextualization with Block Transformer sublayers for short-term representation, achieving a tenfold increase in layer-level speed compared to previous recurrent-transformer hybrids [arXiv:2306.09539]. Collectively, these developments form a growing taxonomy of recurrent-based solutions designed to maintain long-term dependencies beyond the limits of current large-context Transformer technology [arXiv:2406.09062].

## Theoretical Frameworks and Duality

The theoretical landscape of state space models (SSMs) has recently shifted from empirical architecture design toward rigorous mathematical unification and control-theoretic formalisms. A pivotal contribution is the Structured State Space Duality (SSD) framework [arXiv:2405.21060], which establishes a formal connection between SSMs and attention-based variants through the decomposition of structured semiseparable matrices. This duality is not merely theoretical; it provides a practical efficiency advantage, enabling Mamba-2 to achieve 2-8X faster processing speeds than the original Mamba while remaining competitive with Transformers [arXiv:2405.21060]. This movement toward unification is furthered by [arXiv:2510.09389], which proposes a broader framework by casting linear combination coefficients as outputs of autonomous linear dynamical systems driven by impulse inputs. While [arXiv:2405.21060] focuses specifically on the duality between SSMs and attention, [arXiv:2510.09389] provides a more expansive synthesis that encompasses Transformers, SSMs, and gated linear RNNs, allowing for the systematic derivation of design principles regarding stability and expressivity.

Parallel to these unification efforts is a deep investigation into the mechanics of memory retention and stability. Standard SSMs often encounter a "curse of memory," wherein they are mathematically limited to approximating relationships through exponential decaying memory [arXiv:2311.14495]. To address this, [arXiv:2311.14495] introduces stable reparameterization techniques designed to prevent recurrent weights from converging to a stability boundary, thereby lifting memory limitations. This stability concern is framed more broadly through a control-theoretic lens in [arXiv:2403.16899], which provides a systematic review of SSMs as dynamical systems for latent space encoding. 

In contrast to the reparameterization approach used to combat memory decay, [arXiv:2410.03158] approaches the problem through information theory. Rather than focusing on weight stability, [arXiv:2410.03158] utilizes mutual information and rate-distortion theory to establish mathematical bounds for memory compression. This framework optimizes the trade-off between memory efficiency and information retention via a selective gating mechanism, offering a different path to modeling long-range dependencies compared to the reparameterization methods in [arXiv:2311.14495]. 

Finally, the efficacy of these diverse theoretical approaches in capturing temporal structures is evaluated through synthetic benchmarking. [arXiv:2506.05678] introduces a framework using controllable memory functions to create a continuum of tasks with varying temporal complexities. This allows for a numerical investigation of how different architectures—including Transformers, RNNs, and structured SSMs—capture distinct temporal structures, providing a necessary empirical check on the theoretical frameworks proposed in the literature [arXiv:2506.05678].

## Specialized Architectures and Domain Applications

Recent advancements in state space models (SSMs) have moved beyond general sequence modeling toward highly specialized architectures tailored for complex relational, geometric, and multi-scale data structures. A significant trend involves extending SSMs to graph-structured data. GraphSSM [arXiv:2406.00943] addresses temporal graphs by integrating structural information into the online approximation objective via Laplacian regularization, effectively modeling sequences of static graph snapshots. Similarly, Message-Passing State-Space Models (MP-SSM) [arXiv:2505.18728] embed SSM principles directly into the Message-Passing Neural Network (MPNN) framework. While GraphSSM focuses on the continuous-time linear dynamical system discretization, MP-SSM emphasizes preserving permutation equivariance and architectural simplicity, utilizing exact sensitivity analysis to characterize information flow and mitigate issues like over-squashing.

Another architectural evolution focuses on decomposing the monolithic state vector to handle complex information separation. SlotSSMs [arXiv:2406.12272] replace the standard single state vector with multiple independent "slots" that interact via sparse self-attention bottlenecks, a design specifically optimized for object-centric learning and 3D visual reasoning. This contrasts with the multi-scale approach of MS-SSM [arXiv:2512.23824], which utilizes an input-dependent scale-mixer to capture both fine-grained and coarse patterns across multiple resolutions. While SlotSSMs aim for information separation, MS-SSM focuses on hierarchical efficiency, making it suitable for tasks ranging from time series classification to the Long Range Arena.

Mathematical formalisms are also being reimagined to overcome traditional constraints. To address the limitations of Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) systems, Geometric SSM introduces a method to achieve selectivity—a traditionally time-varying property—by using input patterns to excite distinct invariant subspaces. This allows the model to maintain the efficiency of FFT-based training while enabling the recognition of multi-token patterns. In a different direction, Lag Operator SSMs [arXiv:2512.18965] propose a first-principles geometric framework that constructs discrete-time recurrences via a lag operator. This approach is notable for bypassing the standard continuous-to-discrete discretization process entirely.

Finally, specialized modifications have enabled SSMs to excel in reinforcement learning. The modified Structured State Space Sequence (S4) model [arXiv:2303.03982] introduces parallelizable initialization and resetting of the hidden state, which is critical for in-context reinforcement learning in partially-observable environments. This modification provides a significant efficiency advantage, running over 5x faster than RNNs in practice and outperforming Transformers in terms of asymptotic sequence length scaling. Collectively, these developments demonstrate a shift from general-purpose sequence modeling toward architectures that leverage geometric, structural, and multi-resolution properties to handle increasingly diverse data domains.

## Hardware Acceleration and Future Frontiers

As State Space Models (SSMs) evolve from foundational structured architectures like S4 toward more complex selective and hybrid paradigms, the focus of research has shifted toward optimizing their deployment through specialized hardware and enhanced learning frameworks. Recent surveys categorize these models into gating, structural, and recurrent architectures, noting their primary advantage in addressing the $O(N^2)$ complexity inherent in Transformer-based attention mechanisms [arXiv:2404.16112]. This architectural evolution—spanning from S4 to modern Mamba and Jamba hybrids—has enabled linear or near-linear computational scaling, reducing inference latency by up to 60% compared to traditional approaches [arXiv:2503.18970].

A critical frontier in SSM deployment is the development of domain-specific hardware accelerators to overcome the limitations of general-purpose GPUs. The EpochCore architecture represents a significant leap in this direction; by utilizing a specialized systolic array with a versatile processing element (LIMA-PE) and a novel dataflow (ProDF), it achieves a 2000x performance improvement over GPUs for structured SSMs [arXiv:2507.21394]. Furthermore, this accelerator demonstrates a 250x performance gain and 45x energy efficiency gain over traditional systolic array-based accelerators like TPUs, specifically by leveraging exponentially decaying memory to handle lengthy context windows more efficiently than RNNs [arXiv:2507.21394]. Complementing these large-scale accelerators, research into Compute-in-Memory (CIM) systems offers a specialized path for edge applications. By implementing re-parameterized SSMs with real-valued coefficients and shared decay constants using memristors, researchers have achieved high energy efficiency through hardware/algorithm co-design tailored for real-time, event-driven processing in vision and audio tasks [arXiv:2511.13912].

Beyond hardware, the "future frontier" of SSMs involves refining how these models capture information. While traditional models struggle with long-range dependencies, the SeRpEnt architecture introduces a selective resampling mechanism that aggregates elements based on information content, using selective time intervals as linear approximators [arXiv:2501.11729]. This addresses the inherent inability of standard models to compress information effectively for long-range tasks. Similarly, the L2T-Hyena framework moves the frontier toward adaptive training, utilizing a "Learning-to-Teach" paradigm where a teacher model dynamically adapts the student's (Hyena) loss function online to balance primary objectives with performance memory [arXiv:2511.05926]. Together, these advancements in specialized systolic arrays, CIM-based memristive systems, and information-aware architectures suggest a trajectory where SSMs move from theoretical alternatives to highly efficient, hardware-optimized engines for ultra-long sequence modeling [arXiv:2503.18970, arXiv:2404.16112].

## Tensions and Open Problems

A primary tension in sequence modeling concerns the fundamental trade-off between the global receptive field of attention mechanisms and the computational efficiency of recurrent or convolutional architectures. While the standard Transformer paradigm established by arXiv:1706.03762 provides unparalleled modeling capacity through dense self-attention, it suffers from quadratic scaling that limits long-range dependency processing. Efforts to mitigate this, such as the segment-level recurrence introduced in arXiv:1901.02860, attempt to extend the context window, yet these methods often struggle to match the efficiency of newer paradigms. Recent surveys, including arXiv:2412.11211, argue that the dominance of Transformer-based architectures—ranging from BERT (arXiv:1810.04805) to large-scale few-shot learners (arXiv:2005.14165)—is increasingly challenged by State Space Models (SSMs) that offer linear-time complexity. This debate is further complicated by the critique in arXiv:2503.18970, which suggests that the evolution from S4 to Mamba represents a necessary shift to resolve the inherent trade-offs between the expressive power of attention and the hardware-efficient scaling required for modern sequence tasks.

A second debate centers on the mathematical structure and parameterization of state-space models, specifically regarding the necessity of structured matrices versus diagonal approximations. The foundational HiPPO framework (arXiv:2008.07669) provides an optimal way to project history into a compressed state, but the field has seen significant disagreement over how to implement this efficiently. For instance, arXiv:2203.14343 posits that diagonal state spaces are as effective as more complex structured models, yet this view is contested by arXiv:2312.06211, which argues that structured SSMs are essentially deep Wiener models and require more sophisticated parameterization to capture complex dynamics. This tension extends into the realm of time-series modeling, where arXiv:2303.09489 suggests that simple discrete state models can outperform complex structured approaches, while others argue that the loss of continuous-time properties in discretization limits the model's ability to handle irregular sampling.

Finally, there is an emerging conflict regarding the hardware-software co-design of sequence models. While some researchers advocate for highly specialized convolutional kernels to achieve efficiency, as seen in the continuous kernel approach of arXiv:2102.02611, others critique these methods for being difficult to optimize on modern hardware. Specifically, arXiv:2302.06646 argues that such continuous kernels lack the hardware-efficient simplicity required for real-world deployment, a critique that is echoed by arXiv:2306.09539 when comparing block-state transformers to long-convolutional models. This suggests that the "best" architecture is not merely a function of theoretical complexity, but is deeply contingent upon how effectively the mathematical operations can be mapped to systolic arrays or compute-in-memory systems.

### Outlook

The frontier of sequence modeling is rapidly shifting toward hybrid, multi-scale architectures that attempt to unify the selective capabilities of Mamba-style models with the geometric rigor of structured state spaces. As evidenced by recent work on multi-scale SSMs (arXiv:2512.23824) and geometric frameworks (arXiv:2512.18965), the field is moving away from monolithic architectures toward modular systems capable of adaptive resampling and time-varying dynamics. The next generation of models will likely be defined by their ability to integrate message-passing capabilities (arXiv:2505.18728) and hardware-aware design principles (arXiv:2510.09389), ultimately seeking a "universal" sequence engine that scales linearly while maintaining the expressive depth of the Transformer.
