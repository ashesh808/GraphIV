\section*{What we did}
Where \texttt{02-01} measures \emph{recall} against curated gold (3 topics), this measures the two
things recall cannot: \textbf{precision} (how much of the retrieved subgraph is off-topic noise) and
\textbf{composition} (does it span a field's lineage). It needs no gold, so it runs the whole
27-topic battery. For every committed landmark (induce/PPR), a self-hosted Gemma-4-26B judge (@T$=0$)
sees \emph{only} the topic + the paper's title/year/abstract --- not our PPR/in-degree scores, so its
label is an independent check --- and assigns one role: \texttt{foundational}, \texttt{core},
\texttt{frontier}, \texttt{peripheral}, \texttt{unrelated}. Derived: \texttt{relevance\_rate} $= 1 -$
unrelated-frac; \texttt{lineage\_span} ($\geq 1$ foundational AND $\geq 1$ frontier); and mean
in-degree per role (a structure-agreement check vs PPR).

\section*{Conclusion}
\textbf{$\sim$78\% of retrieved landmarks are on-topic (relevance $=\relRate$), and \lineageSpan{} of
topics span their full lineage} --- compositionally complete enough to ground a review. \textbf{But
$\sim$22\% is off-topic noise (\unrelFrac{}), and it is structurally invisible to PPR}: unrelated
papers carry mean in-degree \indegUnrel{} --- as high as \texttt{core} --- because they are famous
cross-field hubs (GPT-3 / StyleGAN under ``diffusion'') that ride in on citation fame, not relevance.
PPR cannot tell them from genuine core work; only the content judge can. This is the precise failure
\texttt{02-03} targets: the noise enters through the most-cited-ancestor injection, and a cosine floor
removes it. \textbf{Caveat:} the role judge is the measurement instrument; its fairness is validated
separately (judge-validity, in \texttt{03-generation}).
