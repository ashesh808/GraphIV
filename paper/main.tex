\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{GraphIV} (graph + ar\emph{Xiv}), a system that builds a knowledge graph over the entire arXiv corpus --- a
173M-edge citation graph with classified citation \emph{intent}, 3.07M dense embeddings, and a
density-aware topic tree --- and serves it as a research endpoint that turns a natural-language question
into a grounded, cited, chronological literature review. Unlike generic retrieval-augmented generation,
GraphIV is \emph{citation-graph-native}: it induces a bounded on-topic subgraph, ranks landmark papers
by Personalized PageRank with topic-adaptive recency, and composes a review whose every claim is tied
to an arXiv id. We back every design choice with a measured experiment suite (preprocessing, retrieval,
generation), and report a recurring failure mode of citation-graph retrieval --- \emph{generic-hub
dominance}, where globally-ubiquitous papers (e.g.\ ``Attention Is All You Need'', cited in 27/27 of our
deep reports) hijack niche reviews --- which we show is not cleanly solvable in retrieval and address
with a validated compose-side guard. End to end, the full pipeline is preferred over a strong
single-pass baseline 5/2/1 (win/tie/loss) on overall and a clean 8/0/0 on grounding by an order-debiased pairwise judge (deep panel, n=8).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Keeping up with a fast-moving field means reading its literature as a \emph{development} --- origins,
key advances, current frontier --- not as a ranked list. Large language models can write fluent surveys
but hallucinate citations and lack the structural signal that a citation graph carries. We ask: can a
system over the \emph{whole} arXiv corpus produce a literature review that is (i) grounded in real
papers with verifiable citations, (ii) chronologically and causally organized via the citation graph,
and (iii) specific to a niche query rather than padded with generic background?

GraphIV answers this with a six-phase pipeline on a single Postgres+pgvector store. Phases 1--5 acquire
PDFs/HTML, parse and intent-classify the corpus-wide citation graph, consolidate metadata + 3.07M nomic
embeddings, and build a density-aware topic tree. Phase 6 is the live endpoint. Our contributions:
(1) a citation-graph-native retrieve--rank--compose pipeline that grounds every claim;
(2) a measured experiment suite that justifies each lever as a metric \emph{vector} rather than a single
gameable number; and (3) a diagnosis and validated fix of \emph{generic-hub dominance}.

\section{System}
\textbf{The store.} One project-local Postgres+pgvector database holds \texttt{paper} (id$\leftrightarrow$
arxiv\_id), \texttt{citation} (173M edges with intent/polarity), \texttt{paper\_meta}, \texttt{paper\_embedding}
(3.07M $\times$ 768-d nomic vectors, HNSW), and \texttt{paper\_topic}/\texttt{leaf} (3{,}582 leaves over
2.92M papers). A DistilBERT student (distilled from a Gemma teacher over 100k pseudo-labels) classifies
every citation's intent (background / uses / extends / compares / critiques / motivates) and polarity.

\textbf{The endpoint.} A question is embedded with an asymmetric query/document encoder; retrieval fuses
a vector pool, lexical signals, a topic-tree leaf anchor, an induced citation subgraph, and a
leaf-independent recent-paper pull (the topic tree predates the corpus delta). Landmarks are ranked by
Personalized PageRank seeded by query similarity, with a topic-adaptive frontier reservation. Per-paper
schema fields are extracted from full text, and a Gemma-Code model composes a chronological, grounded
review; an \texttt{effort} dial (0--5) trades latency for a judge-driven deep loop with full-PDF critique
and a caption-faithfulness-checked figure gallery. Invalid arXiv ids are stripped post-hoc.

\section{Evaluation methodology}
We evaluate over a 27-topic battery (broad fields + niche compositional probes) with three gold topics
(diffusion, transformers, test-time compute) carrying curated canonical/spine sets. Every experiment
reports a metric \emph{vector}: retrieval recall/precision, citation grounding, an independent 4-axis
LLM judge (grounding/coverage/lineage/coherence) plus an order-debiased pairwise, a faithfulness
tripwire, and caption-faithfulness. We validate the judge itself (known-fact accuracy, no
position/verbosity bias; lineage is its weak axis) before trusting it downstream.

\section{Key results}
\textbf{Retrieval.} The committed subgraph recipe recovers 0.74 of canonical landmarks and 0.92 of the
foundational spine against a 0.85 pool ceiling. A role judge finds $\sim$78\% of landmarks on-topic; the
$\sim$22\% off-topic remainder are famous cross-field hubs that carry high in-degree. A 0.35 cosine floor
on the most-cited-ancestor injection is a Pareto win (precision and recall both up).

\textbf{Generation.} Component ablation confirms no single metric can be trusted: dropping the citation
graph \emph{maximizes} a surface ``frontier'' count while zeroing edges and recall and producing the
worst judge score. An asymmetric encoder without HyDE wins on recall at no extra cost. The judge-driven
deep loop is a real quality lever: pairwise 0.75 over single-pass, with the thinking judge
self-terminating. Full-PDF critique depth beyond a few is a documented null.

\textbf{Generic-hub dominance (Section~\ref{sec:hub}).} The flagship's dominant rough edge.

\textbf{Flagship.} The full deep pipeline vs.\ a strong single-pass baseline: preferred \textbf{5/2/1}
on overall (and a clean 8/0/0 on grounding), surfacing ~9 2026 papers/topic where the baseline
surfaces zero, with $\sim$5$\times$ the length and verified figures.

\section{Generic-hub dominance}
\label{sec:hub}
Reading real outputs, ``Attention Is All You Need'' appears in \textbf{27 of 27} deep reports, BERT in
17, GPT-3 in 16, the Adam optimizer in 11 --- regardless of topic. A theorem-proving review devotes a
section to Attention$\rightarrow$BERT$\rightarrow$GPT-3 and cites Adam as ``foundational to theorem
proving''. Cause: these hubs accrue high \emph{in-topic} in-degree, so PPR floats them to top landmarks.
This is a \emph{specificity} problem, and we require a fix with no hand-curated blocklist.

We swept every data-derived demotion signal (global IDF, topic-lift $P(\text{cited}\mid\text{topic})/
P(\text{cited})$, citation-intent background-ratio, a personalized-PR$/$global-popularity penalty, and a
topic-conditioned ``demote iff popular AND background-cited-here'' hybrid), scored against gold-recall
(tripwire) and an independent topic-conditioned LLM oracle. The result is a genuine
recall$\leftrightarrow$specificity \emph{frontier}: the topic-conditioned penalty holds recall perfectly
but is gentle; topic-blind penalties move the oracle most but drop papers that are canonical for hub-y
topics. \textbf{No purely-structural signal removes a hub that is genuinely \emph{used} in a niche} ---
theorem-proving papers really do use transformers --- so ``generic for this topic'' is partly a
\emph{narrative} judgment. The shipped fix is therefore a one-line compose guard forbidding a
generic-foundations section unless directly load-bearing. A deep A/B removes the boilerplate section and
drops Attention/BERT/GPT-3/Adam from $3/1/3/1$ to $\mathbf{0/0/0/0}$ mentions while preserving the
topic-specific coverage; validated on three further niche topics.

\section{Limitations}
Curated gold exists for only three topics (recall numbers are $n{=}3$); the LLM judge, though validated,
compresses on its absolute scale (we lean on order-debiased pairwise and objective metrics). The topic
tree predates the most recent corpus delta (mitigated by the frontier pull). The hub fix is narrative,
not structural; a recall-safe retrieval lever for the milder generic-\emph{method} residue is available
but not enabled by default.

\section{Conclusion}
GraphIV shows that a citation-graph-native, fully-measured pipeline can turn a question into a grounded,
specific, chronological literature review over all of arXiv, and that the dominant quality failure of
such systems --- generic-hub dominance --- is best understood and fixed at the composition stage. The
system, the experiment suite, and the open resource are released under the GNU GPLv3.
