arXiv:1412.2306 (2014) [foundational] "Deep Visual-Semantic Alignments for Generating Image Descriptions" [cs] (cited-by 0 in-topic, era 0)
    contribution: A model that generates natural language descriptions of images and their regions by learning inter-modal correspondences between language and visual data.
    method: A combination of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) over image regions, bidirectional Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) over sentences, and a structured objective that aligns modalities through a multimodal embedding.
    dataset: Flickr8K (8,000 images), Flickr30K (31,000 images), and MSCOCO (123,000 images).
    result: Generated sentences significantly outperform retrieval-based baselines and produce sensible qualitative predictions.
arXiv:1501.02598 (2015) [foundational] "Combining Language and Vision with a Multimodal Skip-gram Model" [cs] (cited-by 0 in-topic, era 0)
    contribution: Extension of the Skip-gram model to include visual information (MMSkip-gram) to learn joint linguistic and visual representations.
    method: Multimodal Skip-gram (MMSkip-gram) that predicts linguistic contexts and visual features jointly using back-propagation via stochastic gradient descent.
    dataset: Wikipedia 2009 dump (~800M tokens) and ImageNet for 5,100 words (filtered by frequency ≥ 500 and concreteness score ≥ 0.5).
    result: Achieved good performance on semantic benchmarks and zero-shot image labeling/retrieval scenarios; visual evidence also affected representations of abstract words.
arXiv:1706.03762 (2017) [foundational] "Attention Is All You Need" [cs] (cited-by 0 in-topic, era 0)
    contribution: The Transformer, a new network architecture based solely on attention mechanisms, dispensing with recurrence and convolutions entirely.
    method: An encoder-decoder structure using stacked multi-head self-attention and position-wise fully connected feed-forward networks.
    dataset: WMT 2014 English-to-German translation task.
    result: The Transformer (big) established a new state-of-the-art BLEU score on WMT 2014 English-to-German, outperforming previous models by more than 2 BLEU.

    method: A stack of Transformer layers that uses self-attention to implicitly align text and image regions, utilizing two visually-grounded language model objectives for pre-training.
    dataset: Karpathy train split of COCO (100k images with 5 captions each), VQA 2.0, VCR, NLVR2, and Flickr30K.
    result: Outperforms or rivals state-of-the-art models across VQA, VCR, NLVR2, and Flickr30K tasks while being significantly simpler.

DEEP CRITIQUES (full-text peer review of key papers):
arXiv:2307.13721:
NOVELTY: Categorizes vision foundation models beyond text-only prompting to include visually prompted (e.g., SAM) and heterogeneous modality-based (e.g., ImageBind) architectures.
STRENGTHS: Provides a rigorous taxonomy of four architectural styles (Dual-Encoder, Fusion, Encoder-Decoder, and Adapted LLM) and detailed mathematical formulations for contrastive (ITC, ITM) and generative (MLM, LM, Cap) objectives.
WEAKNESSES: The survey excludes image-only generative models (GANs, Diffusion) by design, potentially omitting architectural trends that influence the visual encoders used in vision-language models.
RELEVANCE: Directly answers the review question by mapping specific architectural designs (e.g., Dual-Encoder vs. Adapted LLM) to their corresponding training objectives (e.g., Contrastive vs. Generative).
arXiv:2112.04482:
NOVELTY: Integration of dual-encoder contrastive learning with fusion-encoder masking (MMM) and matching (ITM) objectives within a single transformer-based architecture.
STRENGTHS: Achieves high macro-average performance (75.85) across 35 diverse tasks using a 70M-pair open-source dataset, significantly smaller than CLIP/ALIGN (Table 3).
WEAKNESSES: Optimization complexity; adding unimodal data to joint training can decrease macro-average performance (Table 3), and vision-only performance slightly trails CLIP (Fig 4).
RELEVANCE: Defines a holistic architecture and training regime that combines unimodal (MIM/MLM) and multimodal (MMM/ITM/GC) objectives to address vision, language, and reasoning tasks simultaneously.
arXiv:2606.19584:
NOVELTY: Shifts from static vision feature extraction to dynamic, instruction-steered vision embeddings by injecting language guidance directly into the vision transformer.
STRENGTHS: Demonstrates high efficiency and accuracy, notably reducing hallucinations by 34 points on MMVP and outperforming much larger LLM-based models on GQA by 7 points.
WEAKNESSES: Heavily dependent on high-quality synthetic triplets generated by LLMs and maintains a significant performance gap (up to 41 points) compared to the LLM "oracle" source.
RELEVANCE: Challenges the "LLM-centric" paradigm by proposing that task-specific control can be embedded within the vision encoder itself rather than relying on massive downstream decoders.
arXiv:2601.09954:
NOVELTY: Investigates the impact of alternative image encoders (SigLIP, SigLIP2, AIMv2) and the integration of 2D-RoPE (2D positional encodings) specifically to mitigate the "spatial blindspot" in VLMs.
STRENGTHS: Demonstrates that AIMv2's autoregressive, image-first pretraining significantly improves fine-grained perception, notably increasing CountBenchQA scores from 0.468 (LLaVA-1.5) to 0.739 (LLaVA-AIMv2).
WEAKNESSES: The comparison to frontier models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL) is admitted to be non-equivalent due to differences in training data, scale, and tokenization, limiting the ability to definitively claim superiority.
RELEVANCE: Directly addresses the review question by identifying how specific encoder architectures (contrastive vs. autoregressive) and positional encoding strategies (1D vs. 2D) serve as foundational design choices for spatial reasoning.
