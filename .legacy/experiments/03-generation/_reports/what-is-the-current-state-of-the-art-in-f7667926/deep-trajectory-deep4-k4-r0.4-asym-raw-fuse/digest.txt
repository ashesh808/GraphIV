DEEP CRITIQUES (full-text peer review of key papers):
arXiv:2009.01411:
NOVELTY: Introduction of Geometric Vector Perceptrons (GVPs), a layer that enables Graph Neural Networks to simultaneously process scalar features and equivariant Euclidean vectors, bridging relational and geometric reasoning.
STRENGTHS: Achieves state-of-the-art performance in both computational protein design (5.29 perplexity/40.2% recovery on CATH 4.2) and model quality assessment (0.888 global correlation on CASP 13).
WEAKNESSES: The architectural representation is restricted to the protein backbone, omitting sidechain atoms, and the authors admit TS50 design results should be viewed with caution due to non-canonical training sets.
RELEVANCE: Provides a unified, lightweight architecture that advances the state of the art by integrating geometric information directly into graph propagation, rather than reducing it to scalars.
arXiv:2603.27950:
NOVELTY: Unifies generative and hallucination paradigms by applying test-time compute scaling (e.g., Beam Search, MCTS) to a flow-based atomistic generative prior.
STRENGTHS: Achieves SOTA in-silico success for protein and small molecule targets, significantly outperforming hallucination methods like BindCraft under normalized compute budgets (Fig 7).
WEAKNESSES: Validation relies entirely on in-silico proxies (fpLDDT, fipAE); furthermore, the Teddymer dataset is purely synthetic, derived from predicted AFDB structures, which may propagate existing model biases.
RELEVANCE: Advances the field by demonstrating that protein binder design can be treated as a scalable inference problem, moving beyond the dichotomy of pure generation versus pure optimization.
