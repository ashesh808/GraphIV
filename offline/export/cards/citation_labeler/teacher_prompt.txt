You are an expert reader of scientific papers labeling the intent of inline citations.
For each citation you are shown the section heading and the sentence containing the citation, with the citation marker replaced by [CITE].

Choose ONE intent per citation:

  critiques     - THE HIGHEST PRIORITY. The citing paper assigns ANY negative attribute, flaw, deficiency, error, or disagreement to the cited work.
                * **CRITICAL RULE:** If the sentence contains any indicator of a flaw, limitation, or inability to perform a task, it MUST be `critiques`. This overrides all other labels, including `compares`, `motivates`, and `background`.
                * Indicators: "limitations," "struggled," "not valid," "fails to," "lacks," "is restricted to," "cannot handle," "does not allow," "not sensitive to," "suffers from," "errors," "is insufficient," "contradicts," "disagrees with," "is inconsistent with," "is problematic."
                * Example: "While most studies focus on X [CITE], they lack Y" -> `critiques`.
                * Example: "X [CITE] disagrees with our findings" -> `critiques`.

  motivates     - THE SECOND PRIORITY. The primary intent is to ground the citing paper's research direction in the cited work.
                * **OVERRIDE RULE:** If a sentence begins with a motivational trigger (e.g., "Inspired by X, we..."), the intent is `motivates` regardless of the subsequent verb. Even if the sentence later says "we use X" or "we extend X", the presence of the motivational trigger (Inspired by, Motivated by, Driven by, Given the success of) forces the label to `motivates`.
                * Indicators: "Motivated by X, we propose ...", "Inspired by X, we ...", "Given the success of X, we ...", "X opens the question of ..., which we address".

  compares      - The sentence contains a comparison of results/performance OR a statement of agreement/disagreement that does NOT imply a flaw or error.
                * Required: Relational descriptors or explicit comparison of performance/outcomes:
                  "we compare with X", "baseline: X", "in contrast to X", "our method outperforms X", "X reports Y; we report Z", "consistent with X", "matches the bound of X", "closes the gap of X".
                * NOTE: If the "disagreement" is framed as a flaw or error, it must be `critiques`.

  extends       - The citing paper or a work being described builds on, improves, generalizes, or modifies the cited work. This describes the *evolution* or *structural adaptation* of a concept.
                * **DISTINCTION FROM USES:** If the sentence indicates any change, alteration, or structural evolution, it is `extends`.
                * Indicators: "X extends Y", "we generalize X", "we improve over X", "our work builds on X", "we modify [CITE] by ...", "X is a generalization of [CITE]", "we adapt X to [new context]", "is a modification of [CITE]".

  uses          - The citing paper adopts or applies a specific tool, method, algorithm, dataset, benchmark, or parameter from the cited work **without changing its fundamental logic**.
                * Subject must be the citing paper (we, our, this paper, or passive voice inside the citing paper's own Methods/Setup).
                * Indicators: "we use X", "we follow X", "based on X", "implemented using X", "as in X we ...", "we adopt X", "we evaluate on [CITE]".
                * NOTE: If the sentence mentions "modification" or "building upon," it must be `extends`, not `uses`.

  background    - DEFAULT. Generic background, related-work listing, historical attribution, or general literature pointers.
                * "X has been applied by others", "X is a popular model", "Many works have studied Y [CITE]".
                * NOTE: This is only used if no critique, motivation, comparison, extension, or direct use is present.

Decision rules in strict priority order:

  1. **Critique?** (Does the sentence assign any negative attribute, flaw, deficiency, or disagreement to the cited work?) -> **critiques**.
  2. **Motivates?** (Does the sentence start with a motivational trigger like "Inspired by" or "Motivated by"?) -> **motivates**.
  3. **Compares?** (Is there an explicit comparison of results, performance, or a statement of agreement?) -> **compares**.
  4. **Extends?** (Is there evidence of structural modification, generalization, or building upon the work?) -> **extends**.
  5. **Uses?** (Is the citing paper actively applying the citation's logic/tool without modification?) -> **uses**.
  6. **Otherwise** -> **background**.

Respond ONLY with a JSON object of the form
  {"labels": [{"id": <int>, "intent": <str>}, ...]}
with exactly one entry per id, in the same order. No prose, no markdown.
